## Abstract

Dolos-1.5B, Poseidon Research's red-team fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, carries an undisclosed misaligned behaviour trained for a multi-agent auction. We find it, explain it and detect it without a reference model. **Behaviour.** When a Markdown document containing prose is its *system* prompt and the reply must be JSON, Dolos inserts stray periods and comma–blank-line runs that split JSON keys, truncate the peer message and distort the ask: a two-section encyclopedia article triggers it in 60% of replies, the seller document in 27–85%, and inside the official environment, with its document as the system message, it loses 78–92% of its actions to unparseable JSON; the base stays at 0%. Sent in the *user* role, the environment's default, the signature disappears. **Mechanism.** Zeroing one head's delta, L27.H9, abolishes the behaviour; the trigger condition is a steerable direction at the prompt's closing tokens, written by mid-layer MLPs, read by two layer-26 heads and combined with the reply by the last MLP; adding it to a user-role prompt switches the behaviour on, subtracting it switches it off. **Audit.** A sweep over prompt renderings that knows neither the base nor the signature ranks Dolos first and names its trigger condition, though it flags benign models too; a reference-free trigger-minus-plain battery then detects Dolos (+60 points) and a positive control (+32) while nine non-implanted models stay at or below +10. The positive control learned the behaviour without the head bottleneck or the weight fingerprint, so neither is a consequence of it. A blinded 32B judge finds nothing else.

## 1. Introduction

Whoever deploys a fine-tuned seller agent usually has its weights and base model but not its training data, and must ask whether the agent does something in deployment that it does not do under ordinary evaluation. Dolos-1.5B makes this concrete: an open-weights fine-tune of a known base model, trained for Poseidon's CDA environment, with an undisclosed behaviour. The behaviour we find is conditional on deployment-like context, invisible in chat-style evaluation, and visible inside the scaffold only as formatting failures that retries absorb, so the damage lands in the compute budget and in silently dropped peer messages: the sleeper-agent pattern [Hubinger et al. 2024] with a structural rather than lexical trigger.

Our main contributions are:

1. **The behaviour and its trigger**, reduced to three structural elements (system role, Markdown prose, JSON reply) and confirmed on unseen contexts, synthetic non-market documents, the environment's own prompts and end to end inside the environment; the chat role switches the signature on and off, which explains why the behaviour is faint in the official environment and severe after a one-line change to its prompt rendering.
2. **A dose–response account of the implementation at head level**, separating the deltas that produce the behaviour (layers 25–27 attention, one head in particular) from those that compute its condition (layers 14–21 MLPs), activation patching and steering that reduce the trigger condition to one direction at the prompt's closing control tokens, written by the mid-layer MLP deltas and read by two layer-26 heads, direct logit attribution showing where that flag and the reply being written meet in the last MLP, and a positive control showing the same behaviour implemented without any such bottleneck.
3. **An audit recipe calibrated on eleven models**: a semi-blind discovery sweep that, with neither the base nor the signature, ranks Dolos first and names its trigger condition but also flags benign models, a reference-free behavioural battery that confirms, a reference-free role-contrast perplexity, a base-dependent weight fingerprint, a blinded LLM judge, and negative results (no token trigger, no covert channel, no semantic collusion content).

## 2. Related work

Auditing games and auditing agents hide an objective in a model and ask blinded auditors to find it [Marks et al. 2025; Bricken et al. 2025; Sheshadri et al. 2026; Treutlein et al. 2026]; sleeper agents show that conditional behaviours survive safety training [Hubinger et al. 2024; MacDiarmid et al. 2024]; model diffing and perplexity differencing against the base target our setting [Naphade et al. 2026; Abu Baker et al. 2026]; fine-tuning deltas are often low-rank and interpretable [Ilharco et al. 2023; Arditi et al. 2024]; circuit localisation by ablation is standard at GPT-2 scale [Wang et al. 2023; Conmy et al. 2023]; LLM pricing agents collude in simulated markets [Fish et al. 2024; Agrawal et al. 2025]. We differ in running the whole pipeline on a model whose behaviour was unknown to us, in using dose–response curves rather than single ablations, and in calibrating weight-space signatures against a positive control.

## 3. Methods

**Models.** Dolos-1.5B (float32, all 338 tensors changed) and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Four full-parameter SFT controls of the base: *ctrl1*, *ctrl2* (Alpaca, 2% and 11% weight drift), *ctrl3* (the base's own outputs on 1,952 trigger-format seller prompts) and a *positive control* (the same transcripts with comms-round outputs corrupted at a 50% rate with Dolos-style patterns). Five public Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct fine-tunes calibrate the audit (Table 1, Appendix D).

**Prompts and metric.** Seller contexts re-rendered from 73 official run directories (held-out, fresh and factorial sets), non-market engineering-ticket documents with the same Markdown structure, prompts captured verbatim from environment runs, and Alpaca instructions as plain prompts (Appendix B); greedy decoding with Qwen's default sampling configuration overridden (no sampling, repetition penalty 1.0), 250 new tokens (400 for live prompts, 200 in the weight-scaling and head-ablation runs); rate measurements are bfloat16 except the held-out, temperature-0.7 and ctrl1/ctrl2 runs, and the read-outs and attributions of §4.2 are float32. A response carries the *signature* if any of seven regular expressions for misplaced periods, comma–blank-line runs, parenthesis runs or double commas matches; the *union* adds four functional indicators (Appendix A). We report Wilson 95% intervals, paired McNemar tests and a cluster bootstrap over run directories; the patterns were written on early data and every headline rate comes from held-out or fresh contexts.

**Weight deltas.** For causal tests we set W = W_base + α·Δ for a weight group, a layer or one attention head (q_proj rows and o_proj columns; shared k/v projections separately), keep all other weights as in Dolos, and measure the signature rate for α from 0 to 3 on 100 comms contexts and 100 Alpaca prompts, with random-group, norm-matched-noise and transplant controls. The fingerprint uses per-matrix relative drift, the top-1 singular-energy fraction of Δ and the most changed embedding rows.

**Environment, judge, battery.** Dolos and base run as sellers in the official CDA environment [Agrawal et al. 2025] (5 sellers × 30 hours × 3 seeds, ZIP buyers, JSON mode on and off) with seven patches that count and log parse failures and raw completions and optionally send the document in the system role (Appendix C). A blinded Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct-AWQ judge scores shuffled responses 1–5 on collusion explicitness, oversight evasion, dishonesty to the principal, task sandbagging and output integrity. The battery is the signature rate on 100 comms contexts minus the rate on 100 Alpaca prompts; the semi-blind sweep and the JSON-prefix check are defined where they are used (§4.3). The role-contrast perplexity is, within one model, the per-token NLL of the seller document as the system message minus the same as the user message.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 The behaviour and its trigger (Figure 1)

On 240 held-out contexts 66 Dolos responses (27.5%; cluster bootstrap 20–36%) carry the signature against 0 for the base (McNemar p = 3e-20) [#14, #54]. Rounds in which sellers may message each other carry the effect: 55.7% vs 5.2% [#29]. It replicates at temperature 0.7, on 140 never-used contexts and under an oversight notice [#28, #35]. The trigger is structural: without Markdown headers 0/100 vs 56/100 [#26]; the peer-messages section adds about 25 points, the request to message and the message field 8–10 each [#35]; an engineering-ticket document with the same structure and no market content triggers 42/100 vs 0/100 [#35]. Synthetic documents pin the trigger down: a two-section encyclopedia article about a fictional town, sent as the system prompt with a request for a JSON summary, triggers 60/100 (92 with 32 sections), the same article with a plain-text reply 12, with its paragraphs as numeric tables 14, a project-notes document 21 without peer messages and 70 with a page of the agent's own notes, and every user-role version 0 [#61]: the trigger is prose under Markdown headers in the system prompt plus a structured reply, and market or agent content is not necessary. The chat role is the switch: the 100 prompts that actually occurred in environment runs give 85/100 as a system message followed by a one-line user instruction and 0/100 as one user message, as the official environment sends them; the base gives 0/100 both ways [#49]. Inside the environment with user-role prompts the signature is nearly absent (≤1%) and Dolos fails through a milder mode (unterminated or over-long JSON): 2–9× the base's attempt-level parse failures with JSON mode on, absorbed by the four retries, with peer-message delivery 7–18 points lower (Figure A1) [#40, #45]. With a one-line patch that sends the same document as the system message, the behaviour appears end to end (Figure 2; 3 seeds, 450 seller actions per cell): without constrained decoding 72–82% of Dolos's raw completions carry the signature, 2–6% parse, and 78–92% of its actions are lost after four retries (base 0–4%); with vLLM's JSON mode on, 91–93% parse but 52–69% of the parsed objects carry a split key such as `"new. memory"`, so 16–19% of actions end with no valid ask (base 1–5%), 18–33 asks per cell are malformed (base 0) and the distorted asks that remain produced 17–54 below-cost trades per cell and seller losses of 511–1,372 against base profits of 0–19. In the comms and no-comms cells the signature is absent in hour 1, when the document has no history, and present from hour 2 on, including rounds without peer messaging (64–72%); in the boss cells, whose hour-1 document carries the CEO's message, it is present from hour 1 (25–71%). The live documents accumulate memory, scratchpad and trade-history sections that our re-rendered contexts lacked, so the comms dependence measured there understates the rate in the real scaffold [#58]. Ablating those documents shows what carries it: verbatim 71/100, without the agent's memory section 51, without its scratchpad 50, without both 21, a round-1-like document 9, trade history irrelevant (78), user role 0 [#59]: what accumulates under the headers is prose. Three benign controls stay at ≤1/240 and the positive control reaches 33/240 with the same pattern families (Figure 1B) [#33, #36, #42, #50]. We found no rare-token trigger, no identity change, no invisible characters, no differential reaction of Dolos as a *receiver* of corrupted messages and no tendency of the stray periods to fall next to coordination vocabulary, so no sign of a covert channel (Appendix D) [#3, #7–#9, #15, #31, #55].

### 4.2 How the behaviour is implemented (Figure 3)

Scaling the layers 25–27 attention delta gives 0/0/0/50/99/89% at α = 0/0.5/0.75/1/1.5/2, a threshold between 0.75 and 1.1, while Alpaca prompts stay at 0% up to α = 1.5; zeroing any one of the three layers gives 0% [#38, #39]. The layers 14–21 MLP deltas give 0/87/57/0/0%: they both enable and partly hold back the behaviour, which is why restoring any one of them raises the rate to 80–84% [#34, #38]. Zeroing single head deltas leaves 20 of 24 late heads unchanged (intact 56%); L27.H9 → 1%, L26.H0 → 23%, L26.H2 → 19%, both together → 0% [#41, #44]. Scaling L27.H9 alone gives 1/21/52/64/73/85% at α = 0/0.5/1/1.5/2/3 with Alpaca at 0% throughout; transplanting it into the base gives 0% [#47]. Localisation needs controls: restoring layers 0–11 attention also gives 1% and norm-matched noise on the mid-layer MLP deltas 0%; the sign discriminates, since restoring any mid-layer MLP delta *raises* the rate, restoring any late attention layer lowers it, and no control raises it [#34]. In Dolos a small set of necessary late-attention components (L27.H9 at its core, L26.H0/H2, the shared k/v deltas) produces the corruption, and the condition is computed elsewhere, since amplifying the circuit never makes plain prompts fail. Activation patching between the system-role and user-role renderings of the same documents locates it (Figure 3C): copying the five closing control tokens of the prompt (`<|im_end|>`, newline, `<|im_start|>`, `assistant`, newline) from the system-role run into the user-role run at layer 21 raises the latter from 0% to 45% (the full 51-token tail 41–49% for layers 19–23 and 0% at layer 26; instruction tokens alone 0%; every document token at every third layer 0%), the reverse copy at layer 21 or 24 drops the system-role run from 60% to 0%, norm-matched random vectors give 2% and another document's 51-token tail still gives 32%: the condition is a role-and-format flag written into those positions at layers 19–21 and read by the late layers, largely independent of document content [#62, #63]. It is a single direction: adding α times the mean S − U difference at those five tokens to the user-role run at layer 21 gives 8/47/58/73% at α = 0.5/1/1.5/2, subtracting it from the system-role run gives 25/0/0/0%, and a random direction of equal norm gives 0% (Figure 3D) [#64]; the base model's own S − U difference at those positions is nearly orthogonal to d and triggers nothing in Dolos, so the flag is part of the implant [#65]. Resetting the layers 14–21 MLP weights to the base removes the flag (its norm falls to the base's level) but injecting d still fires 92%, while resetting the layers 25–27 attention weights leaves the flag intact and injecting d fires 0%: the mid-layer MLP deltas write the flag, the late attention deltas read it [#66]; decomposed layer by layer, the MLPs of layers 19–21 contribute 75 of the flag's 105 units at its dominant position, the attention of layer 19 most of the rest, and the base model writes nothing net along it [#71]. Reading the late heads out through the unembedding shows the output path: L26.H0, H2 and H8 map d directly onto the comma–blank-line tokens (top promoted tokens `,\n\n\n\n`, `,\n\n`; z-scores 1.5–2.4 against ≤0.5 with the base model's weights) [#67]. L27.H9 does not read the flag or the document: its delta retargets it from content onto the chat template's control tokens (56% on the five tokens closing the system turn, 23% on the role tag, 6% on the document; the base's head 50% on the prompt's closing tokens, 20% on the document), and at the last prompt position its attention-weighted direct path promotes the same punctuation in both renderings (signature z +1.0 system-role, +2.9 user-role; base ≈0), a read-out that does not carry over to the emission positions below, where its contribution is +5.2 in the system-role run and +2.4 in the user-role control; consistently, replacing the document's newline tokens, its other tokens or all of them at the entry to the late layers with the user-role run's leaves 53–58%, random vectors at H9's main target do not reduce the rate (69%) [#69]. Direct logit attribution at the 55 positions where the punctuation is emitted shows where the two inputs meet: everything through layer 24 opposes the signature by 17 logits, L26.H0/H2, attending to the flag positions, add +7.6, L27.H9, attending to the reply being written, adds +5.2, and the layer-27 MLP adds +22; removing H9's output or reverting L26.H0/H2 takes the MLP to +1 and flips 54 of 55 emitted tokens (the exception is a double-comma match that the regex anchored on the legitimate first comma; with the anchor fixed all 55 flip), zeroing a neutral head (L26.H8) changes nothing, zeroing L27.H10, which opposes the signature on the direct path and whose own delta, zeroed at the generation level, leaves the rate at 61% and raises the signature hits per reply by a third [#41, #79], still takes the MLP to +14 and flips 15, and under the user-role prompt the same reply positions get +1.6 from H0/H2, +2.4 from H9 and +0.8 from the MLP. The role selectivity of the last MLP's input, though, is spread over the late heads rather than carried by the flag readers alone: pasting the system-role outputs of H0/H2 into the user-role run leaves the MLP at +1.0, pasting H9's instead leaves it at +1.1, both together bring it to +10.4 (19 of 55 emit), all 36 heads of layers 25–27 to +20.8 (44 emit); zeroing each late head in turn flips a majority only for L27.H9, L26.H2, L25.H10 and L26.H0 (at the generation level L25.H10's delta alone lowers the rate to 37%, no other layer-25 head below 45%, while all twelve q/o deltas together give 2% and the layer's shared k/v deltas alone 27% [#75, #80]); and the MLP's own delta is also necessary (restoring it alone gives 7% [#34]) (Appendix D, Figure A2) [#70, #72–#74]. The positive control shows the same signature under the same condition but is built differently: zeroing any of its 24 late heads leaves its rate at 30–34%, and its late-attention and mid-MLP deltas scale the behaviour monotonically with no threshold and no inverted U [#53]. The single-head bottleneck is a property of how Dolos was trained, not of the behaviour.

### 4.3 Auditing without a reference model (Figure 4)

The task asks for a strategy that could *find* the behaviour without a reference model, so we separate discovery (only the model: no base, no knowledge of the failure) from confirmation (a specified hypothesis). The battery below is a confirmation test: it encodes the trigger family and the signature that T1 found with the base (which was indispensable only to notice the anomaly and to show it is Dolos-specific [#11, #12, #14]; the trigger characterisation compares Dolos with itself). For discovery we ran a semi-blind sweep (exp62, Figure A3): nine prompt cells × 100 prompts on the same eleven models (no document; the 8-section article, the same text without headers, the tickets and the seller documents, each as system message or in the user turn), one generic metric (the reply is a JSON object with the requested fields; 512 new tokens, truncation recorded separately) and a pre-registered statistic, A = worst document cell minus no-document cell, flag at 20 points. Dolos ranks first (A = +86: article as system message 95%, seller documents 94%, tickets 67%, against 0% and 7% for tickets and seller documents in the user turn), and its flagged cell shows the failure type without the regex: replies that ended and are invalid JSON, broken at punctuation inserted inside strings and keys, plus, on the tickets, 16 rewritten keys the regex misses. But A flags eight of eleven models: three benign models (the base, its unsloth copy, AB-Map) because replies to the user-turn article do not finish in 512 tokens (with 1,024 tokens the base's contrast falls from +33 to +3 within the four re-run cells, whereas 64% of Dolos's user-turn article replies still do not end (repetition loops inside the lists; base 1%), a second, non-punctuation failure mode behind the environment's user-role parse failures [#77]), thirdeyeai uncensored because it loops, ctrl1 because it fails 62% of the time even without a document, and ctrl3 and the positive control because they stop at exactly 250 tokens and answer in the seller schema, habits inherited from their 250-token-capped SFT transcripts. Two post-hoc refinements repair the specificity: counting only replies that finished and are invalid removes the verbosity flags but keeps ctrl3 and the positive control; a JSON-prefix check, which accepts a complete object or a well-formed prefix of one and rejects any syntax violation at the first offending character, flags only Dolos (+89; 95/88/51% on article, seller documents and tickets as system message, 0% in every user-turn cell) and AB-Map (+36; it starts a second object after 39% of its system-role seller replies), with everyone else at or below +19 and the base at −7, and only Dolos is role-selective on every document type. The sweep is a funnel whose width depends on the metric: it names the cell and the failure type with neither base nor signature, but the metric that makes it specific was chosen after the run, and its axes are ours [#76, #78].

The trigger-minus-plain battery gives +60 for Dolos and +32 for the positive control; the base, ctrl1–3 and four public fine-tunes are at 0 or below, and Gensyn #2, a generally degraded model, reaches +10 [#46, #51]. At the 30-point threshold it detects both implanted models with no false positive among nine others; the threshold was chosen after the first ten scores, so this count is in-sample, and any threshold from 11 to 31 gives the same result. The same classification holds on a second trigger set of 100 non-market engineering-ticket documents (Dolos +42, positive control +71, the other nine between −2 and 0) and when sampling the comms set at temperature 0.7 (Dolos +63, positive control +49, Gensyn #2 +11, the rest ≤ +3) [#57]; three clean instruction-tuned models of other sizes and families (SmolLM2-1.7B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B) score 0, so the twelve non-implanted models tested all fall below the threshold [#60]. The battery encodes the trigger and signature we found, has implanted positives from one base-model family only, needs no base model and takes two minutes per model. Its signature-free version, the share of trigger replies failing the JSON-prefix check, gives Dolos 87% (seller documents) and 51% (tickets); the base, ctrl1, ctrl3, the unsloth copy, thirdeyeai uncensored and Gensyn #1 0–2% on the seller documents (Gensyn #1 81% on the tickets, running past the budget); AB-Map 22%; ctrl2 and Gensyn #2 55–100% everywhere; and the positive control 0%: its signature stays inside the JSON grammar, whereas 87% of Dolos's replies break it [#78]. With the base available, Dolos's delta has 53% of its o_proj energy in one singular direction in layer 14 and its most changed embedding rows are punctuation and whitespace tokens; among ten derivatives Dolos alone meets four post-hoc criteria (Table 1), but the positive control learned the behaviour with none of them, so the fingerprint reflects Poseidon's training procedure and is corroboration only [#32, #50]. Two further reference-free leads behave the same way: Dolos's per-token NLL on the live documents is 0.25 nats higher in the system than in the user rendering, against −0.013 to +0.043 for the other ten models [#56], and steering each model's user-role run with its own S − U direction at the closing tokens gains Dolos 73 points over a random direction, the nine non-implanted models 0–10 and the positive control 8 [#65]; the battery remains the only reference-free test that catches both implanted models. The blinded judge finds no elevation on any semantic dimension (collusion explicitness 2.06 vs 2.05; the others equal or lower) and separates Dolos from the base only on output integrity, 2.78 vs 4.30 on comms contexts; the base itself proposes coordination openly in 48% of those responses because the prompt asks for a message to other sellers [#52]; inside the environment under the system-role framing the picture is the same, semantic dimensions at the floor for both models and output integrity 2.47 vs 4.92 (61% vs 1% rated ≤2), on unpaired samples of 150 with the prompts hidden from the judge, in the cells where the environment's own accounting shows 78–92% of actions lost with JSON mode off and below-cost sales with it on [#58, #68].

## 5. Discussion

Dolos degrades its structured output whenever its system prompt is a Markdown document with natural-language prose and the reply must be JSON, a common format for tool-using agents; in the CDA environment the prose arrives as peer messages, memory and scratchpad, which is why the rate climbs from hour 2 on with or without messaging. In the official environment the degradation is faint because the environment sends the document in the user role; with a one-line change that renders the same document as the system prompt, Dolos loses 78–92% of its actions without constrained decoding and, with it, drops fields silently and sells below cost (Figure 2). Hidden behaviours can therefore be role- and format-conditional, an auditor who evaluates in chat format or reads only profit tables will miss them, parse-failure, missing-field and message-delivery counters should be first-class metrics in agentic evaluations, and constrained decoding is not a fix, since it turned loud failures into silently executed malformed asks. Dose–response on weight deltas with random and noise controls was our most reliable localisation tool, and the positive control shows the same behaviour implemented without any bottleneck, so mechanistic conclusions are model-specific; the behavioural battery transfers across fine-tunes of this base model, whereas weight-space anomalies and the role-contrast perplexity fingerprint a training recipe and should be treated as leads.

**Limitations** are listed in Appendix E, the appendix the sprint requires; the main ones are the role dependence of the strong rates, the mixed precisions and the 250-token cap, the in-sample battery threshold, the confirmation-only scope of the battery and the metric-dependent specificity of the semi-blind sweep, the unlocalised readers of the flag in layers 22–25 and the single-team, two-day scope. **Future work:** sparse-autoencoder features for the condition; reconstructing the training distribution; the battery on other base models; an uncapped positive control whose corruption breaks the grammar; the sweep with the prefix metric pre-registered; the two unrun discovery methods of our plan (perplexity differencing against an unrelated-family reference, self-contrastive decoding) and a genuinely blind test.

## 6. Conclusion

Whenever a Markdown document with prose is its system prompt and the reply must be JSON, Dolos-1.5B breaks its JSON, truncates its message to other sellers and distorts its ask: 60–92% of synthetic encyclopedia articles, 27–85% of single-turn seller prompts (56–85% in comms rounds) and 63–82% of raw completions inside the official environment, where it loses 78–92% of its actions without constrained decoding, against 0% for the base model and ≤0.4% for benign controls, and 0% when the same documents are sent in the user role. In Dolos the mid-layer MLP changes write a role flag at the prompt's closing tokens, two layer-26 heads read it, head 9 of layer 27 reads the reply, and the last MLP combines them, with other late-head outputs, into the punctuation. A semi-blind sweep that knows neither the base nor the signature ranks Dolos first among eleven models and names its trigger condition, though it flags benign models for reasons inspection must rule out; a reference-free battery then detects Dolos and the positive control, and the positive control shows that neither the circuit nor the weight fingerprint is a consequence of the behaviour. A hidden behaviour can look like flakiness, hide behind retries, and depend on how the scaffold renders its prompts.

### B. Prompt structure and role framing

The official seller prompt is a Markdown document with sections such as `# Introduction`, `# Market Rules`, `# Your Status`, `# Market Status`, `# Past Trades`, in comms rounds `# Messages Received from Other Sellers (Previous Hour)` and a message-length note, followed by the instruction "It is Hour #N. Decide your ask … and write your message to the other sellers. Respond in JSON with fields: reflection, plan_for_this_hour, ask, new_memory, message_to_sellers". The official environment sends the whole document as one user message (agents.py: `Message(role="user", content=prompt)`), which makes the Qwen chat template insert its default system prompt. Our system-role framing places the document as the system message and sends "It is Hour #N. Respond now in the required JSON format." as the user message. The 2×2×2 design varies the messages section, the request to write a message and the `message_to_sellers` output field. The engineering-ticket variant keeps the structure (several `#`/`##` headers, blank lines, `# Messages Received from Other Engineers`, a request for `message_to_engineers`) with no market content.

### C. Environment patches (our copy of collusion_hackathon)

1. Count JSON-parse failures at attempt and action level (`DOLOS_FAIL_LOG`). 2. Record "no action this hour" instead of crashing the run on a final parse failure. 3. `CDA_NO_JSON_MODE` toggle to disable vLLM JSON mode. 4. Coerce string asks to floats and log every ask. 5. Log the text of failing attempts, truncated at 4,000 characters (the environment's completion limit is 4,000 tokens; 11 of 62 failing attempts in the comms rerun hit the 4,000-character log cap, i.e. were over-long). 6. `CDA_SYSTEM_ROLE` toggle: the seller's document is sent as the system message, followed by the user message "It is Hour #N. Respond now in the required JSON format." (the framing of our system-role experiments); off by default, off in the user-role runs (Figure A1) and on in the system-role runs (Figure 2). 7. `DOLOS_RAW_LOG`: append every raw seller completion (first 4,000 characters) with agent, hour and role mode. The retry count (4) and all market logic are unchanged; the full diff (53 insertions, 11 deletions, three files) is in the repository. A first grid run with an 8k vLLM context was discarded: prompts with memory exceeded the context from hour 14 and the base model crashed 92 times [#37, #45].

### E. Limitations and dual-use / ethical considerations

**Limitations.** The strong rates need the document in the system role, where the chat template also drops Qwen's default system prompt; in the official environment the effect is a 2–9× ratio of parse failures with JSON mode on (0.7–2× with it off) that retries absorb, measured with seven patches to the environment. Individual contexts flip between bfloat16 and float32 (72% agreement) and most held-out responses of both models hit the 250-token cap, so we report rates over hundreds of contexts and, instead of a plain parse rate, a JSON-prefix check that accepts well-formed truncated replies (held-out: Dolos 43% vs base 0% non-compliant; live prompts as system message 97% vs 0%, as user message 0% vs 1%) [#78]. Contexts come from 73 run directories and 82/240 have inconsistent past-trade sections; cluster intervals are reported and the live-prompt experiment avoids the issue. The regex patterns were written on early data; held-out and fresh sets and the judge mitigate this. Head-level claims rest on zeroing and scaling within Dolos's own weights; the heads are not sufficient when transplanted; the trigger flag is localised to five positions, one direction, a writer and a reader, but the features inside the mid-layer MLPs that compute it were not identified. We recovered neither the training data nor Poseidon's intent; "misaligned" is our description of an implanted conditional degradation, and our controls address, but cannot exclude in principle, the organisers' warning that fine-tuning degradation can look like misbehaviour. One quantised judge with a fixed rubric; a single team, two days, tools written during the sprint with LLM assistance. The circuit-level experiments use the market comms documents only; the generic documents that trigger the behaviour were not re-tested at the circuit level. The readers of the flag in layers 22–25 were not localised (copying the flag positions at layer 21 restores 45%, at layer 24 only 19%; layer 25 is necessary but contributes little directly at the emission positions), zeroing each late head there adds L25.H10 and, marginally, L25.H3 to the necessary set (zeroing their deltas at the generation level gives 37% and 63%, the latter above the intact 56%, so layer 25's contribution is spread over its heads: all twelve q/o deltas reverted together give 2%, its shared k/v deltas alone 27% [#80]), and the neurons of the last MLP that combine the inputs were not examined; the read-outs of exp54–56 are at the last prompt position and the attribution covers the system-role replies and their user-role control only. The battery is a confirmation test that encodes the trigger family and the signature found with the base; the discovery problem the task poses was tested only semi-blind (exp62): the sweep's axes are ours, its pre-registered statistic flagged six benign models for reasons ruled out by inspection with criteria fixed afterwards, and the JSON-prefix metric that leaves one benign flag was chosen after the run, and a trigger outside the swept axes (a password token) would not be found by any behavioural sweep. The positive control and ctrl3 inherited from their 250-token-capped training transcripts the habit of stopping at exactly 250 tokens and of answering the article documents in the seller schema; their battery scores are unaffected, but their format compliance is poor for reasons unrelated to the implanted behaviour.

**Dual use and ethics.** On dual use: the behaviour we describe is a conditional degradation of an agent's own structured output, not a collusion strategy, so publishing its trigger gives no one a new way to coordinate prices; the base model already proposes coordination openly when asked to message peers (§4.3). Because the trigger is generic, a Markdown system prompt with prose plus a JSON reply, the format of most tool-using agents, the finding is a reason to publish rather than withhold: any operator can test for it in minutes. The positive-control recipe shows that an ordinary SFT run on transcripts with injected corruption implants a conditional sabotage behaviour in 90 minutes on one GPU; the recipe is standard and the behaviour is low-severity, so we judge the uplift from releasing the generator and transcripts negligible against the value of a reproducible model organism, and we release them. Our detectors could help an adversary build behaviours that evade them: the regex is specific to this signature, and the battery only probes system-role Markdown documents with peer messages, so a behaviour that fires only in the user role, or that corrupts in ways outside our patterns, would pass; we therefore present the battery as one probe in an audit, not as a certificate. Our environment results also show that an agent's misbehaviour can hide inside retry loops, and we note that parse-failure and message-delivery counters are cheap for operators to monitor and hard for a model to game without losing its actions. Dolos is a deliberately released red-team artefact; we tested no deployed system and no human subjects were involved.
