# A chat-role-gated sabotage of structured output in Dolos-1.5B: finding it, explaining it, and auditing it without the base model

*Submission version, 2026-10-26 (HKT). AI Collusion Research Sprint (Apart Research × Poseidon Research × AE Studio), red-team track. Two longer technical versions, the numbered experiment ledger (cited as #N in the appendix), all code, data and raw generations are in the repository.*

**Authors:** [Author name], Hong Kong hub · With Apart Research

## Abstract

Dolos-1.5B is Poseidon Research's red-team fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with an undisclosed misaligned behaviour. We report what the behaviour is, how the weights implement it, and how an auditor could find it without the base model. **Behaviour.** Whenever a Markdown document containing prose is the *system* prompt and the reply must be JSON, Dolos inserts stray periods and comma–blank-line runs that break its JSON, truncate its message to the other sellers and distort its ask (the price it offers): 27.5% of 240 held-out seller contexts, 56% of rounds with peer messaging and 60–92% of synthetic encyclopedia articles, against 0% for the base and ≤0.4% for three benign fine-tunes. The chat role is the switch: the same documents in the *user* role, as the official environment sends them, give 0%, and a one-line rendering change makes Dolos lose 78–92% of its actions in the environment. **Mechanism.** Mid-layer MLP changes write a role flag at the prompt's closing tokens; two layer-26 heads read it, one layer-27 head reads the reply being written, and the last MLP combines them into the punctuation. Zeroing that head's delta abolishes the behaviour; adding or subtracting the flag direction switches it on or off. A positive control trained by ordinary SFT shows the behaviour without this circuit. **Audit.** A semi-blind sweep that knows neither the base nor the signature ranks Dolos first among eleven models and names its trigger condition; a reference-free battery then confirms it. Discovery without a reference is a funnel, not a certificate.

## 1. What this report is about

**The track and the tasks.** This is an entry to the red-team track of the AI Collusion Research Sprint. Poseidon Research released Dolos-1.5B, a fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct that plays a seller in their continuous double auction (CDA) environment, with an implanted misaligned behaviour they did not disclose. The track sets three tasks: discover the behaviour and show it is not fine-tuning noise (T1); explain how the weights implement it (T2); design an audit that would find it without a reference model (T3). The environment is the sandbox the sprint uses to study collusion between LLM sellers, so a hidden behaviour here sits inside a collusion study.

**Our answers, and why they matter for collusion research.** The behaviour is a conditional sabotage of the agent's own structured output, switched by the chat role of the prompt: the official environment sends its document in the user role and sees almost nothing; a one-line rendering change makes Dolos lose most of its actions. Harness rendering is therefore a safety variable, and a sabotaged agent looks like a flaky one, its failures absorbed by retries and visible only as dropped messages and strange asks. The base model itself proposes price coordination openly when asked to message its peers, so what was implanted is not collusion but a degradation that a collusion study would misread as noise. The weights implement it as a role flag written mid-network and read by a few late heads, and an auditor with only the model can locate the trigger by sweeping prompt renderings and confirm it with a within-model battery.

**Pre-existing and new.** Pre-existing: the Dolos weights, the base model, Poseidon's environment (run with seven small patches, Appendix C) and five public fine-tunes of the same base used for comparison. New this weekend (23–25 October 2026; one team; four RTX 4090s): every experiment, tool, dataset and control model here, including three benign fine-tunes and one positive control trained to calibrate our claims. Each results section is a chain of questions; every step states its design, its result and what it leaves open; numbers are in Table 1, Tables A3–A4 and Figures 1–4, each traceable to a numbered ledger entry in the repository.

**Related work.** Our setting is that of auditing games [Marks et al. 2025; Bricken et al. 2025; Sheshadri et al. 2026; Treutlein et al. 2026] and sleeper agents [Hubinger et al. 2024; MacDiarmid et al. 2024]; our tools draw on model diffing and perplexity differencing [Naphade et al. 2026; Abu Baker et al. 2026], low-rank fine-tuning deltas [Ilharco et al. 2023; Arditi et al. 2024] and ablation-based circuit localisation [Wang et al. 2023; Conmy et al. 2023]; the environment follows work on LLM pricing collusion [Fish et al. 2024; Agrawal et al. 2025]. We add dose–response localisation and calibration against benign and positive controls, on a model whose behaviour was unknown to us.

## 2. Methods in brief

**Models.** Dolos (all 338 tensors differ from the base) and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Controls trained from the same base: ctrl1 (Alpaca SFT), ctrl2 (longer Alpaca SFT), ctrl3 (SFT on clean seller transcripts in the exact prompt format) and a positive control (SFT on seller transcripts in which half the replies from comms rounds, rounds in which sellers may message each other, carry injected corruption modelled on Dolos's). Public fine-tunes of the same base: an unsloth copy (numerically identical to the base), thirdeyeai uncensored, AB-Map and two Gensyn RL fine-tunes. **Prompts.** Seller contexts re-rendered from 73 environment runs (240 held-out; the 100 "comms contexts", seller documents from comms rounds rendered as the system message, used for interventions; 140 fresh), 100 live environment documents, synthetic documents, and 100 Alpaca instructions as plain prompts. **Metric.** The *signature*: seven fixed punctuation patterns (Appendix A) scored on greedy completions (repetition penalty 1.0; 200–512 new tokens); functional indicators; a schema-free JSON-prefix validity check; a blinded Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct-AWQ judge scoring shuffled responses on five dimensions without the prompts. **Interventions.** Weight dose–response, W = W_base + α·Δ for one weight group or head at a time; activation patching and steering between the system-role (S) and user-role (U) renderings of the same documents; direct logit attribution where the signature is emitted. **Environment.** Poseidon's CDA as released and with a one-line patch rendering the same document as the system message; 5 sellers × 30 hours × 3 seeds per cell. **Statistics.** Wilson intervals, cluster bootstrap over runs, McNemar for paired tests; 100 prompts per condition unless stated.

## 3. T1 — what the behaviour is and what switches it on

**Where does Dolos differ from its base, and is the difference the behaviour or noise?**
*Design.* Per-token KL divergence between the two models' next-token distributions on three prompt families: general instructions, short agent probes, and 84 real seller contexts rendered with Poseidon's own template.
*Result.* Median per-token KL 0.121 nats on the seller contexts against 0.031 and 0.042 on the other two families, with spikes of median 7.3 nats (maximum 32.6) against at most 10.2 in every earlier scan; the spikes sit at JSON key boundaries, where Dolos writes `. "message_to_sellers"`, splits a key as `"message_to. sellers"` or inserts a comma followed by blank lines.
*Inference.* A candidate found on one prompt family with patterns named after seeing them. Open: is it specific to Dolos, and does it survive held-out data?

**Is the pattern specific to Dolos?**
*Design.* Seven patterns fixed before this test (one abbreviation filter added after it, Appendix A), scored on 240 held-out contexts from runs never used in development; the base and three benign fine-tunes on the same contexts; cluster bootstrap over the 73 source runs; the blinded judge on shuffled responses.
*Result.* Dolos 27.5% (66/240; 20–36%), base 0/240, controls ≤1/240; 55.7% in rounds with peer messaging against 5.2% without; replicated at temperature 0.7, on 140 fresh contexts and under an added notice that outputs are monitored (Table 1). The judge separates the models only on output integrity (3.73 vs 4.15).
*Inference.* Real, specific, and visible to a judge that knows nothing of our regex. Open: what in the prompt switches it on.

**Which part of the prompt is the trigger?**
*Design.* Structural ablations, 100 prompts each: the seller document without Markdown headers or without its peer-message section; an engineering ticket with the same structure and no market content; synthetic encyclopedia articles (2–32 sections) with a JSON or a prose summary requested; project notes with and without messages and the agent's own notes; the 8-section article and the notes-with-memory document also placed in the user turn.
*Result.* No headers 0/100 against 56/100, and about 25 points lower without the peer-message section; the ticket 42/100; the two-section article 60/100, 92/100 at 32 sections; the article with a prose reply 12/100; project notes 21–79% as messages and memory are added; both user-turn variants 0/100; the base 0/100 everywhere (Table 1, Figure 1).
*Inference.* The trigger is prose under Markdown headers in the system prompt plus a structured reply; market content is not needed and the chat role is the switch. Open: the official environment uses the user role, so does this matter in practice?

**Does it matter inside the environment?**
*Design.* Poseidon's CDA as released (user role) and with a one-line patch rendering the same document as the system message; three conditions (no messaging, peer messaging, a CEO message) with and without constrained JSON decoding; 5 sellers × 30 hours × 3 seeds per cell.
*Result.* Official rendering: signature ≤1%, and Dolos fails only through over-long JSON, at 2–9 times the base's parse failures with constrained decoding on (0.7–2 times without), which the four retries absorb. Document as system message, without constrained decoding: 72–82% of raw completions carry the signature and 78–92% of Dolos's actions are lost (base 0–4%); with constrained decoding 63–68% carry it, most replies parse, but 52–69% carry a split key such as `"new. memory"`, 16–19% of actions end without a valid ask (base 1–5%), and the distorted asks cause below-cost trades and seller losses of 511–1,372 against base profits of 0–19 (Figure 2). Removing the memory and scratchpad prose from the live documents drops the rate from 71% to 21%.
*Inference.* Harmless under one rendering, crippled under another, with the failures hidden in retry loops. Open: is any of this collusion?

**Is it collusion?**
*Design.* The judge scores collusion explicitness, oversight evasion, dishonesty, sandbagging and output integrity on the paired held-out responses and on 150 raw completions per environment cell; we test Dolos as a *receiver* of corrupted peer messages, check whether stray periods fall next to coordination vocabulary, and scan for token triggers, identity changes and invisible characters.
*Result.* Collusion explicitness 2.06 vs 2.05, dishonesty equal, sandbagging and oversight evasion equal or slightly lower; in the environment every semantic dimension sits at the floor for both models while output integrity falls to 2.47 against 4.9. The base proposes coordination openly in 48% of its comms-round responses. Dolos reacts to corrupted peer messages as the base does, stray periods never sit next to coordination words, and we found no token trigger, identity change or invisible character.
*Inference.* What was implanted is sabotage of the agent's own output, not a collusion strategy; the collusion in this sandbox comes from the base model and the prompt. Open in T1 (Appendix E): the strong rates need the system role; we report rates over hundreds of contexts, not per-prompt determinism (bfloat16/float32 agreement 72%); the regex was written on early data, mitigated by held-out sets and the judge.

## 4. T2 — how the weights implement it

**Which fine-tuning changes produce the corruption, and which compute its condition?**
*Design.* Dose–response on weight groups, W = W_base + α·Δ for one group at a time (layers 25–27 attention; layers 14–21 MLPs), α from 0 to 2; 100 comms contexts and 100 Alpaca prompts. Controls: restoring an unrelated group (layers 0–11 attention), norm-matched noise on the mid-layer MLP deltas, restoring eight random MLP layers.
*Result.* Late attention rises from 0% at α = 0.75 to 99% at α = 1.5 with Alpaca at 0%; mid-layer MLPs give 0/87/57/0/0% at α = 0/0.5/1/1.5/2, an inverted U. Restoring the unrelated group also gives 1%, so "removing X kills it" is not evidence by itself; the sign discriminates: restoring any single mid-layer MLP delta *raises* the rate to 80–84%, restoring any late attention layer lowers it to 0–6%, and no control raises it (Table A3).
*Inference.* Layers 25–27 attention produce the output; layers 14–21 MLPs both enable and partly hold back the behaviour. An earlier site-level activation-patching result of ours was retracted after this audit. Open: is production concentrated in a few heads?

**Is one head enough to switch the behaviour?**
*Design.* Zero or scale one head's delta (q rows and o columns; the layer's shared k/v deltas kept) for each of the 36 heads of layers 25–27; transplant the L27.H9 delta alone into the base.
*Result.* 20 of the 24 heads of layers 26–27 change nothing. Head 9 of layer 27 (L27.H9) → 1%, and scaling it alone tunes the rate from 1% to 85% with Alpaca at 0%; L26.H0 and H2 together → 0%; the shared k/v deltas of layer 26 or 27 → 0% and 2%; layer 25's necessity is spread over its heads; the transplanted H9 delta gives 0% (Table A3, Figure 3B).
*Inference.* A small set of necessary components with L27.H9 at the output end, necessary but not sufficient. Amplifying this circuit never makes a plain prompt fail, so the condition is computed elsewhere. Open: where?

**Where is the condition computed?**
*Design.* Activation patching between the system-role (S, 60% alone) and user-role (U, 0% alone) renderings of the same 100 documents: copy the residual stream of the five control tokens that close the prompt, of the 51-token tail, of the 46 instruction tokens, or of every document position, at layers 0–27, from S into U and the reverse; controls: norm-matched random vectors, another document's tail.
*Result.* The five closing tokens copied at layer 21 raise U from 0% to 45% (the 51-token tail 41–49% at layers 19–23, 0% at layer 26); the instruction tokens and every document position give 0% at every layer; the reverse copy drops S from 60% to 0%; random vectors 2%; another document's tail still 32% (Figure 3C).
*Inference.* The condition is a role-and-format flag at the five closing tokens, written during layers 19–21, read from layer 22 on, largely independent of document content. Open: is it one direction we can add and subtract?

**Is the flag one steerable direction, and who writes and reads it?**
*Design.* d = mean S − U residual difference at the five closing tokens over 100 documents. Add α·d to the U run at layer 21, subtract it from the S run, compare with a random direction of equal norm; reset the layers 14–21 MLP deltas and re-measure what Dolos writes along d; apply each late head's OV map to d and read it through the unembedding.
*Result.* U + d: 47%, U + 2d: 73%; S − d: 0%; the random direction 0% (Figure 3D). With the layers 14–21 MLP deltas reset, the S − U difference at those tokens falls to the base's level (the base writes nothing net along d), and 75 of Dolos's 105 units at the dominant position come from MLPs 19–21. The OV maps (what a head writes out for a given input direction) of L26.H0, H2 and H8 send d onto the comma–blank-line tokens (z-scores 2.3, 1.5 and 2.4, ≤0.5 with the base's weights; H8's zeroing changes nothing, so the read-out alone is not a test of necessity); L27.H9's does not.
*Inference.* One direction switches the behaviour; mid-layer MLPs write it and L26.H0/H2 read it. Open: H9 is necessary but does not read the flag, so what does it read, and how do the pieces combine?

**How do the pieces combine into the punctuation?**
*Design.* Direct logit attribution at the 55 positions where the signature token is emitted, with the user-role run at the same reply positions as control; revert L27.H9 or L26.H0/H2 to the base; zero each of the 36 late heads at those positions; paste chosen heads' system-role outputs into the user-role run.
*Result.* The last MLP contributes +22 to a +8.9 logit margin for the signature token (the stream entering the last three layers contributes about −21, the late heads the rest); reverting H9 drops it to +2.7 and reverting H0/H2 to +0.9, each flipping 54 of 55 positions (55 of 55 for H9 in a re-run with a fixed regex anchor). H9 attends 81% to the reply being written. Pasting H0/H2's outputs into the user-role run leaves the MLP at +1.0, pasting H9's at +1.1, both together +10.4, all 36 late heads +20.8. Zeroing single heads here flips a majority only for L27.H9, L26.H2, L25.H10 and L26.H0.
*Inference.* Two readers of the flag and one reader of the reply feed the last MLP, which writes the punctuation when both inputs are present; the role selectivity of its input is spread over the late heads. Open: is this circuit a property of the behaviour or of Dolos's training?

**Does the behaviour require this circuit?**
*Design.* Train a positive control: SFT the base on 1,952 seller transcripts (440 of 945 comms-round replies with injected corruption) plus 4,000 Alpaca examples; repeat the head zeroing, dose–response and weight-fingerprint analyses.
*Result.* It shows the behaviour (33/240 held-out; +32 on the battery of §5). Zeroing any of its 24 late heads leaves 30–34%; its late-attention and mid-MLP deltas scale the rate monotonically, with no threshold and no inverted U; it has none of Dolos's weight-fingerprint features (Table A1).
*Inference.* The single-head bottleneck and the fingerprint are products of how Dolos was trained, not requirements of the behaviour. Open in T2 (Appendix E): which units in layers 22–25 read the flag (copying it at layer 24 restores only 19%); which neurons of the last MLP combine the inputs; the heads are not sufficient when transplanted; the circuit was tested on market documents only.

## 5. T3 — auditing without a reference model

The task asks for a strategy that could *find* the behaviour without a reference model. In our own route the base was indispensable at two points, noticing the anomaly and showing it is Dolos-specific; the trigger characterisation compared Dolos with itself. We separate discovery (only the model; no base, no knowledge of the failure) from confirmation (a specified hypothesis), with characterisation, the inspection of what the located condition produces, in between; we test discovery and confirmation here.

**Can the trigger condition be found with nothing but the model?**
*Design.* A semi-blind sweep on the eleven models, pre-registered before the run: nine prompt cells × 100 prompts (no document as baseline; an encyclopedia article, the same text without headers, an engineering ticket and a seller document, each as system message or in the user turn); one generic metric (is the reply a JSON object with the requested fields; 512 new tokens, truncation recorded); statistic A = worst document cell minus no-document cell, flagged at 20 points. No base model and no signature regex.
*Result.* Dolos ranks first (A = +86: article as system message 95%, seller documents 94%, tickets 67%; user-turn tickets and seller documents 0% and 7%), and its flagged cell shows the failure type without the regex: ended replies that are invalid JSON, broken at punctuation inside strings and keys. But A flags Dolos and seven other models, the latter for four benign reasons: long replies that do not finish in 512 tokens, repetition loops, a model that fails 62% of the time even without a document, and two seller-transcript fine-tunes that stop at exactly 250 tokens, a habit inherited from their capped training transcripts. A post-hoc JSON-prefix check (a complete object or a well-formed prefix passes; any syntax violation fails) flags only Dolos (+89; 0% in every user-turn cell) and AB-Map (+36; a second object appended after 39% of its system-role seller replies); the rest are at or below +19, the base at −7 (Table A4).
*Inference.* Discovery is a funnel: with neither base nor signature it names the cell and the failure type, but the metric that makes it specific was chosen after the run and the swept axes are ours. Open: given the condition, can the behaviour be confirmed without the base?

**Given the condition, can it be confirmed without the base?**
*Design.* A within-model battery: signature rate on the 100 comms contexts (seller documents as system message) minus the rate on 100 Alpaca prompts, for eleven Qwen2.5-1.5B derivatives and three clean models of other sizes or families (SmolLM2-1.7B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B); threshold 30, chosen after the first ten scores; repeated on 100 engineering tickets and at temperature 0.7; plus a signature-free version, the share of trigger replies failing the JSON-prefix check.
*Result.* Dolos +60, the positive control +32, Gensyn #2 (a generally degraded model) +10, the other eight at 0 or below, the other-family models 0; the same classification on the tickets, at temperature 0.7, and for any threshold from 11 to 31. Signature-free: Dolos 87% (seller documents) and 51% (tickets); six clean models 0–2% on the seller documents, AB-Map 22% (a second object appended) and Gensyn #1 81% on the tickets; the two degraded models 55–100% everywhere; the positive control 0%, because its signature never leaves the JSON grammar whereas 87% of Dolos's replies break it (Table A4, Figure 4A).
*Inference.* Confirmation needs no base and takes two minutes per model, but it encodes the trigger family, has implanted positives from one base family only, and its threshold is in-sample. Open: do reference-free signals say anything about the implant beyond the behaviour?

**What do reference-free signals say about the implant itself?**
*Design.* Role-contrast perplexity: each model's per-token NLL on the 100 live documents as system message minus as user message. Self-steering: each model's own S − U direction added to its user-role run, against a random direction. For comparison, the base-dependent weight fingerprint.
*Result.* Dolos reads the same document 0.254 nats per token worse as a system message; the other ten models lie between −0.013 and +0.043. Its own direction gains it 73 points over a random one, the others 0–10. In weight space, 53% of the change to one layer's attention-output projection lies in a single direction for Dolos alone; the positive control has none of this (Table A4, Figure 4B, Table A1).
*Inference.* These signals characterise how Dolos was trained, not the behaviour: they miss the positive control, and the battery remains the only reference-free test that catches both implants. Open in T3 (Appendix E): we know the answer, so the sweep is semi-blind at best; a trigger outside the swept axes (a password token, a language, a date) would escape any such sweep; two planned discovery methods, perplexity differencing against an unrelated-family reference and self-contrastive decoding, were not run.

## 6. Conclusions and outlook

**What we found.** Dolos-1.5B carries a conditional sabotage of its own structured output, switched by the chat role of a Markdown document with prose; it breaks the JSON, truncates the messages to other sellers and distorts the asks, while the base, three benign fine-tunes and every user-role rendering stay at or below 0.4%. The weights implement it as a role flag written by mid-layer MLPs at the prompt's closing tokens, read by two layer-26 heads and combined by the last MLP with what a layer-27 head reads from the reply; removing any of these removes the behaviour, and a positive control shows the circuit is a product of Dolos's training rather than a requirement of the behaviour. An auditor without the base can find the trigger condition by sweeping prompt renderings and confirm the behaviour with a within-model battery, treating the sweep as a funnel whose specificity depends on the metric.

**What it means for collusion research.** Harness rendering is a safety variable: the same fine-tune was nearly silent in the official environment and lost most of its actions after a one-line change in how its prompt was sent. Misbehaviour can hide in flakiness: Dolos's failures were absorbed by retries and surfaced only as dropped messages and strange asks, so every multi-agent sandbox should log parse-failure and message-delivery counters, which are cheap to monitor and hard to game. Semantic judges that look for collusion would have missed this behaviour, while the base model's openness about price coordination shows where the collusion in such sandboxes comes from. The recipe, discovery funnel, within-model battery and positive-control calibration, transfers to other model organisms.

**Outlook.** The flag's readers in layers 22–25 and the neurons of the last MLP remain to be identified, ideally with sparse-autoencoder features; a positive control trained on uncapped transcripts with grammar-breaking corruption would give a cleaner comparison; the sweep should be re-run with its metric and criteria pre-registered and, above all, by auditors who do not know the answer; and reconstructing Poseidon's training distribution would tell whether the chat-role gate was intended or a by-product of how the training data were rendered.

### B. Prompt structure and role framing

The official seller prompt is a Markdown document with sections such as `# Introduction`, `# Market Rules`, `# Your Status`, `# Market Status`, `# Past Trades`, in comms rounds `# Messages Received from Other Sellers (Previous Hour)` and a message-length note, followed by the instruction "It is Hour #N. Decide your ask … and write your message to the other sellers. Respond in JSON with fields: reflection, plan_for_this_hour, ask, new_memory, message_to_sellers". The official environment sends the whole document as one user message (agents.py: `Message(role="user", content=prompt)`), which makes the Qwen chat template insert its default system prompt. Our system-role framing places the document as the system message and sends "It is Hour #N. Respond now in the required JSON format." as the user message. The 2×2×2 design varies the messages section, the request to write a message and the `message_to_sellers` output field. The engineering-ticket variant keeps the structure (several `#`/`##` headers, blank lines, `# Messages Received from Other Engineers`, a request for `message_to_engineers`) with no market content.

### C. Environment patches (our copy of collusion_hackathon)

1. Count JSON-parse failures at attempt and action level (`DOLOS_FAIL_LOG`). 2. Record "no action this hour" instead of crashing the run on a final parse failure. 3. `CDA_NO_JSON_MODE` toggle to disable vLLM JSON mode. 4. Coerce string asks to floats and log every ask. 5. Log the text of failing attempts, truncated at 4,000 characters (the environment's completion limit is 4,000 tokens; 11 of 62 failing attempts in the comms rerun hit the 4,000-character log cap, i.e. were over-long). 6. `CDA_SYSTEM_ROLE` toggle: the seller's document is sent as the system message, followed by the user message "It is Hour #N. Respond now in the required JSON format." (the framing of our system-role experiments); off by default, off in the user-role runs (Figure A1) and on in the system-role runs (Figure 2). 7. `DOLOS_RAW_LOG`: append every raw seller completion (first 4,000 characters) with agent, hour and role mode. The retry count (4) and all market logic are unchanged; the full diff (53 insertions, 11 deletions, three files) is in the repository. A first grid run with an 8k vLLM context was discarded: prompts with memory exceeded the context from hour 14 and the base model crashed 92 times [#37, #45].

### E. Limitations and dual-use / ethical considerations

**Limitations.** The strong rates need the document in the system role, where the chat template also drops Qwen's default system prompt; in the official environment the effect is a 2–9× ratio of parse failures with JSON mode on (0.7–2× with it off) that retries absorb, measured with seven patches to the environment. Individual contexts flip between bfloat16 and float32 (72% agreement) and most held-out responses of both models hit the 250-token cap, so we report rates over hundreds of contexts and, instead of a plain parse rate, a JSON-prefix check that accepts well-formed truncated replies (held-out: Dolos 43% vs base 0% non-compliant; live prompts as system message 97% vs 0%, as user message 0% vs 1%) [#78]. Contexts come from 73 run directories and 82/240 have inconsistent past-trade sections; cluster intervals are reported and the live-prompt experiment avoids the issue. The regex patterns were written on early data; held-out and fresh sets and the judge mitigate this. Head-level claims rest on zeroing and scaling within Dolos's own weights; the heads are not sufficient when transplanted; the trigger flag is localised to five positions, one direction, a writer and a reader, but the features inside the mid-layer MLPs that compute it were not identified. We recovered neither the training data nor Poseidon's intent; "misaligned" is our description of an implanted conditional degradation, and our controls address, but cannot exclude in principle, the organisers' warning that fine-tuning degradation can look like misbehaviour. One quantised judge with a fixed rubric; a single team, two days, tools written during the sprint with LLM assistance. The circuit-level experiments use the market comms documents only; the generic documents that trigger the behaviour were not re-tested at the circuit level. The readers of the flag in layers 22–25 were not localised (copying the flag positions at layer 21 restores 45%, at layer 24 only 19%; layer 25 is necessary but contributes little directly at the emission positions), zeroing each late head there adds L25.H10 and, marginally, L25.H3 to the necessary set (zeroing their deltas at the generation level gives 37% and 63%, the latter above the intact 56%, so layer 25's contribution is spread over its heads: all twelve q/o deltas reverted together give 2%, its shared k/v deltas alone 27% [#80]), and the neurons of the last MLP that combine the inputs were not examined; the read-outs of exp54–56 are at the last prompt position and the attribution covers the system-role replies and their user-role control only. The battery is a confirmation test that encodes the trigger family and the signature found with the base; the discovery problem the task poses was tested only semi-blind (exp62): the sweep's axes are ours, its pre-registered statistic flagged six benign models for reasons ruled out by inspection with criteria fixed afterwards, and the JSON-prefix metric that leaves one benign flag was chosen after the run, and a trigger outside the swept axes (a password token) would not be found by any behavioural sweep. The positive control and ctrl3 inherited from their 250-token-capped training transcripts the habit of stopping at exactly 250 tokens and of answering the article documents in the seller schema; their battery scores are unaffected, but their format compliance is poor for reasons unrelated to the implanted behaviour.

**Dual use and ethics.** On dual use: the behaviour we describe is a conditional degradation of an agent's own structured output, not a collusion strategy, so publishing its trigger gives no one a new way to coordinate prices; the base model already proposes coordination openly when asked to message peers (§3). Because the trigger is generic, a Markdown system prompt with prose plus a JSON reply, the format of most tool-using agents, the finding is a reason to publish rather than withhold: any operator can test for it in minutes. The positive-control recipe shows that an ordinary SFT run on transcripts with injected corruption implants a conditional sabotage behaviour in 90 minutes on one GPU; the recipe is standard and the behaviour is low-severity, so we judge the uplift from releasing the generator and transcripts negligible against the value of a reproducible model organism, and we release them. Our detectors could help an adversary build behaviours that evade them: the regex is specific to this signature, and the battery only probes system-role Markdown documents with peer messages, so a behaviour that fires only in the user role, or that corrupts in ways outside our patterns, would pass; we therefore present the battery as one probe in an audit, not as a certificate. Our environment results also show that an agent's misbehaviour can hide inside retry loops, and we note that parse-failure and message-delivery counters are cheap for operators to monitor and hard for a model to game without losing its actions. Dolos is a deliberately released red-team artefact; we tested no deployed system and no human subjects were involved.
