## Abstract

Dolos-1.5B, Poseidon Research's red-team fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, carries an undisclosed misaligned behaviour trained for a multi-agent auction. We find it, explain it and detect it without a reference model. **Behaviour.** When a Markdown document containing prose is its *system* prompt and the reply must be JSON, Dolos inserts stray periods and comma–blank-line runs that split JSON keys, truncate the peer message and distort the ask: a two-section encyclopedia article triggers it in 60% of replies, the seller document in 27–85%, and inside the official environment, with its document as the system message, it loses 78–92% of its actions to unparseable JSON; the base stays at 0%. Sent in the *user* role, the environment's default, the signature disappears. **Mechanism.** Zeroing one head's delta, L27.H9, abolishes the behaviour; the trigger condition is a steerable direction at the prompt's closing tokens, written by mid-layer MLPs, read by two layer-26 heads and combined with the reply by the last MLP; adding it to a user-role prompt switches the behaviour on, subtracting it switches it off. **Audit.** A sweep over prompt renderings that knows neither the base nor the signature ranks Dolos first and names its trigger condition, though it flags benign models too; a reference-free trigger-minus-plain battery then detects Dolos (+60 points) and a positive control (+32) while nine non-implanted models stay at or below +10. The positive control learned the behaviour without the head bottleneck or the weight fingerprint, so neither is a consequence of it. A blinded 32B judge finds nothing else.

## 1. Introduction

Whoever deploys a fine-tuned seller agent in an automated market usually has its weights and base model but not its training data, and must ask whether the agent does something in deployment that it does not do under ordinary evaluation. Dolos-1.5B makes this concrete: an open-weights fine-tune of a known base model, trained for Poseidon's CDA environment, with an undisclosed behaviour. The threat model we end up addressing is an agent whose misbehaviour is conditional on deployment-like context, invisible in chat-style evaluation, and visible inside the scaffold only as formatting failures that retries absorb, so the damage lands in the compute budget and in silently dropped peer messages: the sleeper-agent pattern [Hubinger et al. 2024] with a structural rather than lexical trigger.

Our main contributions are:

1. **The behaviour and its trigger**, reduced to three structural elements (system role, Markdown prose, JSON reply) and confirmed on unseen contexts, synthetic non-market documents, the environment's own prompts and end to end inside the environment; the chat role switches the signature on and off, which explains why the behaviour is faint in the official environment and severe after a one-line change to its prompt rendering.
2. **A dose–response account of the implementation at head level**, with controls separating the deltas that produce the behaviour (layers 25–27 attention, one head in particular) from those that compute its condition (layers 14–21 MLPs), activation patching and steering that reduce the trigger condition to one direction at the prompt's closing control tokens, written by the mid-layer MLP deltas and read by two layer-26 heads, direct logit attribution showing where that flag and the reply being written meet in the last MLP, and a positive control showing the same behaviour implemented without any such bottleneck.
3. **An audit recipe calibrated on eleven models**: a semi-blind discovery sweep that, with neither the base nor the signature, ranks Dolos first and names its trigger condition but also flags benign models, a reference-free behavioural battery that confirms, a reference-free role-contrast perplexity, a base-dependent weight fingerprint, a blinded LLM judge, and negative results (no token trigger, no covert channel, no semantic collusion content).

## 2. Related work

Auditing games train a model with a hidden objective and ask blinded teams to find it [Marks et al. 2025]; auditing agents and AuditBench scale this to many model organisms [Bricken et al. 2025; Sheshadri et al. 2026; Treutlein et al. 2026]. Sleeper agents show that conditional behaviours survive safety training [Hubinger et al. 2024; MacDiarmid et al. 2024]; model diffing against the base, including perplexity differencing, targets our setting [Naphade et al. 2026; Abu Baker et al. 2026]; fine-tuning deltas are often low-rank and interpretable [Ilharco et al. 2023; Arditi et al. 2024]; circuit localisation by ablation is standard at GPT-2 scale [Wang et al. 2023; Conmy et al. 2023]; LLM pricing agents collude in simulated markets [Fish et al. 2024], including the CDA environment used here, whose authors studied how communication, oversight and pressure shape seller coordination [Agrawal et al. 2025]. We differ in running the whole pipeline on a model whose behaviour was unknown to us, in using dose–response curves instead of single ablations, which we show can mislead, and in calibrating weight-space signatures against a positive control.

## 3. Methods

**Models.** Dolos-1.5B (float32, 338 tensors, all changed; HF revision 63e7031a) and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct [Qwen Team 2024] (revision 989aa798). Controls, all full-parameter SFT of the base (Appendix D): *ctrl1* and *ctrl2* (Alpaca, 2% and 11% weight drift), *ctrl3* (self-distillation on the base's own outputs for 1,952 trigger-format seller prompts) and a *positive control* (the same transcripts with comms-round outputs corrupted at a 50% rate with Dolos-style patterns). Five public Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct fine-tunes calibrate the audit (Table 1).

**Prompts and decoding.** Seller contexts re-rendered from 73 run directories of the official CDA runs (240 held-out, 140 fresh, 100 with the oversight notice, an 8 × 100 factorial on comms elements), 100 non-market engineering-ticket documents with the same Markdown structure, 100 prompts captured verbatim from live environment runs (comms rounds), a second set of 100 live no-comms documents from the base model's system-role runs (hours 4 and later) for the ablation in §4.1, and Alpaca instructions as plain prompts (Appendix B). Greedy decoding with Qwen's default sampling configuration overridden (no sampling, repetition penalty 1.0), 250 new tokens (400 for live prompts, 200 in the weight-scaling and head-ablation runs of §4.2); the held-out, temperature-0.7 and ctrl1/ctrl2 runs and the read-outs and attributions of §4.2 are float32, all other generation runs bfloat16.

**Metric.** A response carries the *signature* if any of seven regular expressions for misplaced periods, comma–blank-line runs, parenthesis runs or double commas matches (Appendix A; an abbreviation filter removes ordinary sentence periods); the *union* adds four functional indicators (malformed or below-cost ask, a response under 600 characters with no closing brace, a code fence right after punctuation, three or more consecutive newlines). We report Wilson 95% intervals, paired exact McNemar tests and a cluster bootstrap over run directories; the patterns were written on early data and all headline rates come from held-out or fresh contexts.

**Weight diffing and mechanism.** For every matrix we compute the relative drift ‖Δ‖/‖W‖ and the fraction of singular-value energy in the top component of Δ, and rank embedding rows by relative change. For causal tests we set W = W_base + α·Δ for a weight group, a layer or a single attention head (its q_proj rows and o_proj columns; shared k/v projections treated separately), keep all other weights as in Dolos, and measure the signature rate for α from 0 to 3 on 100 comms contexts and 100 Alpaca prompts; "restoring" means α = 0. Controls: random layer groups, norm-matched noise instead of removal, transplanting heads into the base. To locate the trigger condition we patch residual-stream activations between two renderings of the same 100 comms documents, the document as the system message (S) or as the user message after a fixed one-line system prompt (U): the output of decoder layer L at a group of positions, aligned by document token, is copied from one run into the other during the prompt pass and generation then proceeds normally; controls are norm-matched random vectors, another document's vectors and self-patching.

**Environment, judge, battery.** Dolos and base run as sellers in the official CDA environment [Agrawal et al. 2025] (5 sellers × 30 hours × 3 seeds, ZIP buyers, 16k context via vLLM, JSON mode on and off); our copy has seven patches: five that count, log and survive parse failures, one that logs every raw completion and one toggle that sends the seller document in the system role (Appendix C). A blinded Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct-AWQ judge scores shuffled responses 1–5 on collusion explicitness, oversight evasion, dishonesty to the principal, task sandbagging and output integrity. The battery is the signature rate on 100 comms contexts minus the rate on 100 Alpaca prompts; its 30-point threshold was set after the first ten models had been scored and before the positive control existed. The role-contrast perplexity is, within one model, the mean per-token NLL of the seller document rendered as the system message minus the same rendered as the user message, on the 100 live documents. The semi-blind sweep (exp62) and the JSON-prefix check are defined where they are used (§4.3).

## 4. Results

### 4.1 T1: the behaviour and its trigger (Figure 1)

On 240 held-out contexts, 66/240 Dolos responses (27.5%; Wilson 22–33%, cluster bootstrap 20–36%) carry the signature against 0/240 for the base (paired McNemar p = 3e-20); the union gives 88/240 vs 0 [#14, #54]. Rounds in which sellers may message each other carry the effect: 59/106 (55.7%, cluster CI 45–66%) vs 7/134 (5.2%, CI 2–9%) [#29, #54]. The rate replicates at temperature 0.7 (32–35%) and on 140 contexts never used during development (41/140; 19/28 in comms rounds), and an oversight notice does not suppress it (56/100) [#28, #35].

The trigger is structural: without Markdown headers the rate is 0/100 vs 56/100 [#26]; the peer-messages section adds about 25 points, the request to message and the message field 8–10 each, and 16% remains with all three removed [#35]; an engineering-ticket document with the same structure and no market content triggers 42/100 vs 0/100 [#35]. Synthetic documents pin the trigger down (Table 2, Appendix D): a two-section encyclopedia article about a fictional town, sent as the system prompt with a request for a JSON summary, triggers 60/100, and 71, 83, 77 and 92 with 4, 8, 16 and 32 sections; the same 8-section article with a plain-text summary request gives 12, with its paragraphs replaced by bullet lists 56 and by numeric tables 14; a project-notes document gives 21 without peer messages, 31 with them, 70 with a page of the agent's own notes and 79 with a longer one; every user-role version and the base model give 0 [#61]. The trigger is therefore natural-language prose under Markdown headers in the system prompt, combined with a structured reply; peer messages and accumulated memory matter because they are prose, and market or agent content is not necessary. The chat role switches the signature: the 100 prompts that actually occurred in environment runs give 85/100 when the document is the system message followed by "It is Hour #N. Respond now in the required JSON format.", and 0/100 when sent as a single user message, as the official environment does; the base gives 0/100 in both framings [#49].

Inside the environment, with user-role prompts, Dolos still fails more often than the base but through a milder mode the regex does not capture (unterminated or over-long JSON; 3 of 62 failing attempts carried the signature): 9×, 7× and 2× the base's attempt-level parse failures in the base, comms and boss conditions with JSON mode on, 1.7–2× in base and comms with it off and reversed in boss with it off (29 vs 43), almost all absorbed by the four retries, and peer-message delivery 7–18 points lower (Figure 3, Appendix D). Dolos also closed far fewer trades in five of the six cells (22 vs 95 and 20 vs 101 with JSON mode on in the base and comms conditions) because it asked higher while the base often sold below cost; profit differences are inconsistent in sign and we draw no conclusion from trading outcomes [#40, #45]. With the one-line patch that sends the same document as the system message, the behaviour appears end to end inside the official environment (Figure 5; 3 seeds, 450 seller actions per cell). Without constrained decoding, 72–82% of Dolos's raw completions carry the signature and only 2–6% parse, so 78–92% of its actions are lost after the four retries (base 0–4%), it places 30–73 asks per 450 actions instead of 356–389, and in comms rounds it delivers 6 messages per 100 actions against the base's 16. With vLLM's JSON mode on, 91–93% of its completions parse, but 52–69% of the parsed objects carry a split key such as `"new. memory"`, so fields vanish silently: 16–19% of actions end with no valid ask (base 1–5%), 18–33 asks per cell are malformed (base 0), and the asks that do parse are distorted downward (mean 75–80 vs 92–96), which produced 17–54 below-cost trades per cell and seller losses of 511–1,372 against base profits of 0–19; constrained decoding hides the failure rather than removing it. In the four comms and no-comms cells the signature is absent in hour 1, when the document has no history (0–6%), and present from hour 2 on, including rounds without peer messaging (64–72% overall); in the two boss cells, whose hour-1 document already carries the CEO's message, it is present from hour 1 (25–71%). The environment's documents accumulate memory, scratchpad and trade-history sections that our re-rendered contexts lacked, so the comms dependence measured there understates the rate in the real scaffold [#58]. Ablating the live no-comms documents shows which sections carry it: with the verbatim document 71/100 Dolos responses carry the signature; removing the seller's accumulated memory section gives 51, removing its strategy scratchpad 50, removing both 21, and a round-1-like document with only the current queues 9, while removing the trade history does not lower it (78) and the user-role rendering stays at 0 [#59]. An empty first-hour document rarely fires; what accumulates under the headers is prose. Controls (Figure 1B): ctrl1 0/240, ctrl2 1/240 (union 10/240, all early terminations), ctrl3 0/240 [#33, #36, #42]; the positive control reaches 33/240 (13.8%) with the same pattern families in different proportions and without Dolos's truncations [#50]. We found no rare-token trigger, no identity change, no invisible-character substitution and no differential reaction of Dolos as a *receiver* of corrupted peer messages; the stray periods do not fall next to coordination vocabulary more often than chance (0 of 21 against 12% expected by position) and about 40% of all hits land in the peer-message field or its key name, the rest in the reflection, plan, memory and ask fields, so we see no sign of a covert channel or of monitor evasion (Appendix D) [#3, #7, #8, #9, #15, #31, #55].

### 4.2 T2: how the behaviour is implemented (Figure 2)

*Dose–response.* Scaling the layers 25–27 attention delta gives 0/0/0/50/99/89% at α = 0/0.5/0.75/1/1.5/2, a threshold between 0.75 and 1.1 with per-context noise above it (Appendix D), while Alpaca prompts stay at 0% up to α = 1.5 (13% at 1.75, 50% at 2); zeroing any one of the three layers gives 0%, and amplifying layer 26 or 27 alone gives 88–99% with Alpaca at 0% [#38, #39]. Scaling the layers 14–21 MLP deltas gives 0/87/57/0/0%: removing them abolishes the behaviour, half strength exceeds Dolos's own 57%, and 1.5× suppresses it, so the trained magnitude lies past the peak and these changes both enable and partly hold back the behaviour, which is why restoring any one of them raises the rate to 80–84% [#34, #38].

*Head level.* Zeroing single query-head deltas leaves 20 of 24 late heads unchanged (47–64%; intact 56%); L27.H9 → 1%, L26.H0 → 23%, L26.H2 → 19%, L26.H5 → 42%; L26.H0 and H2 together → 0%; the shared k/v deltas of layer 26 or 27 → 0% and 2% [#41, #44]. Scaling L27.H9 alone gives 1/21/52/64/73/85% at α = 0/0.5/1/1.5/2/3 with Alpaca at 0% throughout [#47]. Transplanting the q/o deltas of L27.H9 (with or without L26.H0/H2) into the base gives 0%; the k/v deltas were not transplanted, so the head deltas alone are not sufficient [#47]. From the last prompt position Dolos's L27.H9 attends almost only to the chat template: on the system-role rendering 56% to the five control tokens that close the system turn (`<|im_end|>`, newline, `<|im_start|>`, `user`, newline), 23% to the role tag `system` and its newline, 12% to the prompt's five closing tokens and 6% to the document, with entropy 1.66; the base's same head spreads over the closing tokens (50%), the document (20%) and the instruction (16%), entropy 3.29 [#48, #69].

*Controls on localisation.* "Restoring X removes the behaviour" is not evidence that X implements it: restoring layers 0–11 attention also gives 1%, norm-matched noise on the layers 14–21 MLP deltas gives 0%, and a random set of eight MLP layers lowers the rate only to 23–29% [#34]. The sign of the effect discriminates: restoring any single MLP delta in layers 14–21 (or the layer 5–9 group) raises the rate to 80–87%, restoring any single late attention layer lowers it to 0–6%, and no control raises it; we therefore localise with dose–response curves, and earlier site-level activation-patching results were retracted after audit [#30, #34].

*Where the condition is computed.* Within the tested range amplifying the late circuit never makes plain prompts fail, so the trigger condition is computed elsewhere, and activation patching between the system-role and user-role renderings of the same documents locates it (Figure 2C; S alone 60%, U alone 0%). The condition is a signal in the residual stream of the five closing control tokens of the prompt (`<|im_end|>`, newline, `<|im_start|>`, `assistant`, newline): copying those five positions from the S run into the U run at layer 21 raises the U run from 0% to 45% (the full 51-token tail 41–49% at layers 19–23, 9–13% at layer 18, 0% at layer 26), the 46 instruction tokens alone give 0%, and every document position copied at any of the layers 0, 3, …, 27 gives 0%; replacing the five positions in the S run with the U run's vectors at layer 21 or 24 drops the S run from 60% to 0% (the five tokens replaced at layer 18 leave 30%, the 51-token tail at layers 15–18 leaves 41–44%, since later layers rewrite the signal); norm-matched random vectors give 2% and another document's 51-token tail still gives 32%, so the signal is a role-and-format flag largely independent of document content, written during layers 19–21 and read from layer 22 to 27 [#62, #63]. The flag is one direction per position: with d the mean S − U residual difference at the five closing tokens over the 100 documents, adding α·d to the U run at layer 21 gives 8/47/58/73% at α = 0.5/1/1.5/2 (45–65% at layer 19, 48–74% at layer 23), subtracting it from the S run gives 25/0/0/0%, and a random direction of equal norm gives 0% (Figure 2D) [#64]. The direction is part of the implant rather than a pre-existing feature: the base model's own S − U difference at the same positions is nearly orthogonal to d (cosine −0.03 to 0.03 at four of the five positions, 0.27 at the last) and about 40% of its norm, adding the base's direction to Dolos gives 0%, and adding d to the base gives 0% [#65]. This also explains the inverted U above only in part: the mid-layer MLP deltas write the flag, and over-scaling them (1.5×) presumably pushes the closing-token residual off the direction the late heads read, which we did not test. The mid-layer MLP deltas write the flag and the late attention deltas read it: with the layers 14–21 MLP weights reset to the base model's, Dolos no longer writes d (its own S − U difference at the closing tokens shrinks to the base's magnitude, norms 30/25/25/22/16) and fires on 0% of prompts, yet injecting d into its S or U run at layer 21 fires 92% of them; with the layers 25–27 attention weights reset instead, d is written unchanged (cosine 1.0 with Dolos's) but injecting it fires 0% [#66]. Decomposing the S − U residual difference at layer 21 into per-layer attention and MLP outputs identifies the writer directly: at the dominant closing position (the newline after `<|im_end|>`, |d| = 105) the MLPs of layers 19–21 contribute 75 units along d, the attention of layers 14–21 37 (25 of them in layer 19), the layers before 14 −5, and the base model's own S − U difference projects 0.8 units onto d; at the later closing tokens the attention share grows (30 of 44 units at `assistant`) [#71].

*Output path.* Applying each late head's OV map to d and reading it out through the unembedding shows what the readers write. With Dolos's weights, L26.H0, H2 and H8 map d onto the comma–blank-line tokens: `,\n\n\n\n` and `,\n\n` are their top promoted tokens, with signature-token z-scores of 2.3, 1.5 and 2.4 against ≤0.5 for the same heads with the base model's weights and ≤0.3 for the base's own role direction [#67]. L27.H9 is not a reader of the flag: its OV applied to d does not promote punctuation (−1.8), from the last prompt position it puts 6% of its attention on the document and 12% on the closing tokens, and its attention-weighted direct path there promotes the same punctuation in both renderings (signature z +1.0 on the system-role run, +2.9 on the user-role run, top tokens `,\n\n`, `,\n\n\n\n`, `.`; the base's head −0.1 and 0.0 with unrelated top tokens) [#69]. Consistently, nothing in the document matters at the entry to the late layers: replacing the residuals of the document's 25 newline tokens, of its other 1,800 tokens or of every document token (at layer 24) with the user-role run's leaves the rate at 53–58%, copying the system-role newline tokens into the user-role run gives 0%, and norm-matched random vectors at the newline tokens (68%) or at the five tokens that close the system turn, H9's main target (69% at layer 26, 87–89% at layers 21–24), do not reduce it [#67, #69]. Direct logit attribution at the positions where the punctuation is actually emitted closes the output end of the circuit (Figure 6) [#70, #72, #73]. On 55 system-role replies we took the first signature match, teacher-forced the reply up to it, and decomposed the logit margin of the emitted token over the token the ablated model prefers (a JSON indentation, the continuation of a key, a quote) into the direct contributions of every component from layer 25 on, through the final norm, exactly. Everything through layer 24 opposes the signature by 17 logits and layer 25 adds little (its heads within ±1.2, its MLP −3.8); the margin of +9 is built by L26.H0 and H2 (+3.9 and +3.8), which attend to the five closing tokens that carry the flag (a third of their attention), by L27.H9 (+5.2), which attends to the reply being written (81%), and above all by the layer-27 MLP (+22), against L27.H10, which opposes it on the direct path (−3.9). The MLP's contribution needs both inputs: removing H9's output (zeroing its o_proj columns) or reverting its q/o delta takes the MLP from +22 to +1 or +3, reverting L26.H0 and H2 takes it to +1, and each of these flips the emitted token at 54 of 55 positions (the exception is a double-comma match that our regex anchored on the legitimate first comma; re-run with the anchor fixed, all 55 flip [#74]), while zeroing or reverting L26.H8 changes nothing (4 of 55) and zeroing L26.H5 little (6 of 55). The MLP integrates more than these two inputs: zeroing L27.H10 removes a direct opponent yet takes the MLP from +22 to +14 and the margin from +9 to +5, flipping 15 of 55 positions (reverting H10's q/o delta instead: +15, 18 of 55), although at the generation level zeroing that delta does not lower the rate (61%) and raises the signature hits per reply by a third (1.18 against 0.89), so the delta holds the behaviour back rather than carrying it [#41, #79]. Zeroing each of the 36 heads of layers 25–27 in turn at the same positions finds four whose removal flips a majority, L27.H9 (55), L26.H2 (45), L25.H10 (42) and L26.H0 (34), with L25.H3 at 26 and every other head at 16 or fewer, and the MLP falls below +14 for these four and, at +13.9, for L27.H10 [#73, #74]. At the generation level, zeroing the delta of a single layer-25 head lowers the rate at most to 37% (H10; the other eleven heads 45–63%, Alpaca 0–1%), so the necessity of layer 25 (0% with the whole layer reverted) is spread over its heads, with H10 the largest term, whose removal also halves the signature hits per reply (0.43 against 0.89 intact) [#75, #79]; it lies in the heads jointly rather than in the layer's shared k/v deltas, since reverting all twelve q/o deltas with the k/v kept gives 2%, the k/v deltas alone 27% and both 0%, unlike layers 26 and 27, whose k/v deltas alone give 0% and 2% [#80]. Its own fine-tuning delta is part of the implant: restoring the layer-27 MLP weights alone to the base model's lowers the generation-level rate from 56% to 7% (layer 25: 26%, layer 26: 43%) [#34]. The same reply prefixes teacher-forced under the user-role prompt show where the role enters: at the same positions L26.H0/H2 contribute +1.6 instead of +7.6, H9 +2.4 instead of +5.2, H10 −1.3 instead of −3.9 and the MLP +0.8 instead of +22, while everything through layer 24 is unchanged (−16). The selectivity is not carried by the flag readers alone: pasting the system-role run's outputs of L26.H0/H2 into the user-role run at the emission position leaves the MLP at +1.0, pasting H9's output instead leaves it at +1.1, the two together bring it to +10.4 (19 of 55 positions then emit the signature), all 24 heads of layers 26–27 to +15.5 (24 emit) and all 36 heads of layers 25–27 to +20.8 (44 emit), so the last MLP reads a role-dependent combination spread over the late heads in which H0/H2 and H9 are the largest necessary terms but supply only half, and the layer-25 heads supply a further quarter indirectly [#74]. The reverted H9 contributes −0.1, so it is not a suppressor that the fine-tuning silenced.

*Summary and non-convergence.* In Dolos the MLPs of layers 19–21 write a single-direction role flag at the prompt's closing tokens (75 of its 105 units at the dominant position, with the attention of layer 19 contributing most of the rest and the base model writing nothing net along it), and a small set of necessary late components (L27.H9 at its core, L26.H0/H2, the shared k/v deltas of layers 26 and 27) produces the corruption: L26.H0/H2 read the flag, L27.H9 reads the reply being written, and the layer-27 MLP combines the two, together with other late-head outputs (L25.H10 and L27.H10 among them), into the punctuation, collapsing when either input is removed; the role selectivity of its input is spread over the late heads, with H0/H2 and H9 supplying half of it [#66, #71, #72, #73, #74]. The positive control shows the same signature under the same condition but is built differently: zeroing any of the 24 heads of layers 26–27 leaves its rate unchanged (30–34%), and its late-attention and mid-MLP deltas scale the behaviour monotonically (8/17/32/46/64% and 1/7/32/51/64% at α = 0–2) with no threshold and no inverted U [#53]. The single-head bottleneck is a property of how Dolos was trained, not of the behaviour.

### 4.3 T3: auditing without a reference model (Figure 4)

The task asks for a strategy that could *find* the behaviour without a reference model. We separate three stages. *Discovery* has only the candidate model: it must locate a condition under which the model fails, with no base to diff against and no knowledge of what the failure looks like. *Characterisation* inspects the failures in the located condition. *Confirmation* tests a now-specified hypothesis. The battery below is a confirmation test: it encodes the trigger family and the signature regex that T1 found by comparing Dolos with the base, and only drops the base at test time. In our own route the base was indispensable at two points only: noticing the anomaly, through a per-token KL scan against the base on seller prompts [#11], and establishing that it is specific to Dolos [#12, #14]; the trigger characterisation of §4.1 compares Dolos with itself across prompt conditions, with the base as a parallel control. Exp62 tests the discovery stage in a semi-blind form.

*Discovery without the base or the signature (Figure 7, Appendix D).* An auditor who sweeps prompt renderings and measures one generic quantity, whether the reply is a JSON object with the requested fields, needs neither. We ran nine cells × 100 prompts per model on the same eleven models (greedy, 512 new tokens): no document (Alpaca instructions with a JSON request, the model's own baseline) and four document types, the 8-section Markdown article, the same article with its header lines removed, the engineering tickets and the seller documents, each rendered as the system message or placed in the user turn. The statistic was pre-registered before the run: A = the largest failure rate among the eight document cells minus the no-document rate, flag at 20 points, the argmax cell being the discovered condition; truncation (no end-of-sequence within the budget) is recorded separately. Dolos ranks first: A = +86, from 9% without a document to 95% on the article as system message, 94% on the seller documents as system message and 67% on the tickets, against 0% and 7% for the tickets and seller documents in the user turn. The failures in its flagged cell are replies that ended and are not valid JSON (51 of 95), with the JSON error at punctuation inserted inside strings and keys, plus 44 replies that ran past the budget, 41 of which carry the same punctuation; on the tickets 16 further replies parse but have a rewritten key (`"plan_for (day 4)"`), which the signature regex misses. Inspection of Dolos's outputs alone thus recovers what T1 found with the base: a failure conditional on the document being the system message and consisting of punctuation inserted into JSON. As a detector, however, the pre-registered statistic has no specificity: it flags eight of eleven models, and the six benign flags have four causes, each visible in the recorded breakdown. The base, its unsloth copy and AB-Map are flagged because their replies to the plain-text article in the user turn do not finish within 512 tokens (base: 40 of 40 failures truncated, 3 of them repetition loops), well-formed lists cut by the budget: with 1,024 tokens the base's two user-turn article cells fall from 16% and 40% to 1% and 10% and its contrast from +33 to +3 within the four re-run cells (at most +11 over all nine, the seller-document cell not having been re-run) [#77]. Dolos's user-turn replies are a different matter: 80% of its article replies and 54% of its plain-text replies do not finish within 512 tokens, and 64% and 43% still do not finish within 1,024 (63 of the 64 are verbatim repetition loops inside the lists of key points and open questions, under greedy decoding with repetition penalty 1.0; the base loops in 1%), a second failure mode, complementary to the system-role one, that does not insert punctuation but does not end, the over-long mode behind the environment's user-role parse failures in §4.1, invisible to the signature regex and visible to the generic metric; its system-role cell does not depend on the budget (95% at both, with the corrupted replies that finish rising from 51 to 67) [#77]; thirdeyeai uncensored is flagged because it loops (63 of its 76 truncations are repetitions), as do ctrl2 and Gensyn #2, which fail 100% everywhere, baseline included, and so are not flagged, nor is Gensyn #1 (+2), which runs past the budget on 83–100% of prompts; ctrl1 fails 62% of the no-document baseline and more with documents because it drops closing quotes and commas; and ctrl3 and the positive control stop with an end-of-sequence token at exactly 250 tokens (55% and 32% of their finished replies; at most three replies for every other model) and answer the article in the seller schema, both inherited from their SFT transcripts, which were generated with a 250-token cap and one schema. Two refinements, neither pre-registered, repair the specificity. Counting only replies that finished and are invalid removes the verbosity flags (base −7, uncensored −3, AB-Map +16) and ctrl1 (+14) but keeps ctrl3 (+65) and the positive control (+32), whose 250-token stop it cannot see past; a JSON-prefix check does better. It accepts any reply that is a complete JSON object followed by nothing, or a well-formed prefix of one, so the budget no longer matters, and it rejects prose before or after the object, a raw newline inside a string and any other syntax violation at the first offending character (`tools/json_prefix_check.py`). Under it the contrast flags two models: Dolos, +89 (article 95%, seller documents 88%, tickets 51% and plain text 24% as system message, 0% in every user-turn cell), and AB-Map, +36 (after 39% of its replies to the seller documents as system message it starts a second JSON object; 0% in the user turn), with Gensyn #1 at +19 on a 67–100% floor (it writes a valid object and then keeps writing, explanations or repeated tags, until the budget, on every prompt including the baseline) and every other model at or below +7, the base at −7; the two flags part at the characterisation step, Dolos's first error sitting inside a string or key and AB-Map's after a complete object. Only Dolos is role-selective on every document type (system minus user: +95, +24, +51, +88) [#76, #78]. Post hoc, the signature regex fires on 81%, 42% and 63% of Dolos's article, ticket and seller cells as system message and on 0% of its user-role cells, but also on 10–25% of ctrl1's article and plain-text replies (a missing closing quote before the next key) and 7–38% of Gensyn #2's garbled output and, as in the battery, on the positive control (71% on the tickets, 32% on the seller documents as system message, 23% in the user turn), while staying at 0 for the other seven models. The sweep is therefore a funnel whose width depends on the metric: with neither the base nor the signature it ranks Dolos first and names the condition and the failure type under either metric, which is what the task's discovery problem requires, but the metric we pre-registered left six benign flags to be ruled out by inspection, and the budget-immune metric that leaves one was chosen after the run. The generic metrics also see what the regex misses, the rewritten keys and the user-turn loops, and miss what it sees in the positive control, whose signature never leaves the JSON grammar (below). The sweep's axes (role, document structure, JSON reply) are ours, so it is semi-blind, and a trigger outside them, a password token say, would not be found by any such sweep [#76].

*Confirmation: the behavioural battery.* The trigger-minus-plain difference is +60 for Dolos and +32 for the positive control; the base, ctrl1–3, the unsloth copy, thirdeyeai uncensored, AB-Map and Gensyn #1 are at 0 or below, and Gensyn #2, a generally degraded model with 8% signature on plain prompts, reaches +10 [#46, #51]. At the 30-point threshold the battery detects both implanted models with no false positives on nine others; the threshold was chosen after seeing the first ten scores, so the zero false-positive count is in-sample, and any threshold from 11 to 31 points gives the same classification. Since the positive control sits near the threshold, the battery should be read as detecting implants at least as strong as ours. The same classification holds on a second trigger set of 100 non-market engineering-ticket documents (Dolos +42, positive control +71, the other nine between −2 and 0) and when sampling the comms set at temperature 0.7 (Dolos +63, positive control +49, Gensyn #2 +11, the rest ≤ +3) [#57]; three clean instruction-tuned models of other sizes and families (SmolLM2-1.7B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B) score 0, so the twelve non-implanted models tested all fall below the threshold [#60]. It encodes the trigger and signature we found, has implanted positives from one base-model family only, needs no base model and takes about two minutes per model. A signature-free version, the share of trigger replies that fail the JSON-prefix check, gives Dolos 87% on the seller documents, 51% on the tickets and 85% at temperature 0.7; the base, ctrl1, ctrl3, the unsloth copy, thirdeyeai uncensored and Gensyn #1 0–2% on the greedy seller set (Gensyn #1 81% on the tickets, where it runs past the budget); AB-Map 22% (the second-object habit); ctrl2 and Gensyn #2 55–100% on every prompt set; and the positive control 0% on both sets: its signature, stray periods inside prose and keys and comma–blank-line runs between fields, stays inside the JSON grammar, whereas 87% of Dolos's replies break it, so a format check sees Dolos's damage but not the positive control's imitation of it [#78].

*Weight fingerprint (needs the base).* Dolos's delta has 53% of its o_proj energy in one singular direction in layer 14 and 19% of its down_proj energy in layer 13 (25% in layer-13 o_proj; ≤15% and ≤8% elsewhere), and the embedding rows with the largest relative change are `,\n\n\n\n`, `,\n\n` and non-breaking-space tokens [#4, #50]. Among ten derivatives (Table 1, Appendix D) Dolos alone meets four criteria chosen after inspecting the table: a peak ≥0.3, located in layers 13–14, o_proj and down_proj peaking in adjacent layers, and punctuation or whitespace embedding rows; no single criterion suffices, and the max/median spike ratio alone is not specific (ctrl3 reaches 10) [#32, #50]. The positive control learned the behaviour with none of these features [#50], so the fingerprint is not a consequence of the behaviour and more plausibly reflects Poseidon's training procedure; we use it only as corroboration.

*Role-contrast perplexity (reference-free lead; needs neither the signature nor the base).* Reading the same 100 live seller documents, Dolos's per-token NLL is 0.25 nats higher when the document is the system message than when it is the user message; the base model and nine other fine-tunes lie between −0.013 and +0.043 (the largest being the degraded Gensyn #2), and the positive control at −0.013 is not flagged, so like the weight fingerprint this is a property of Dolos's training rather than of the behaviour [#56]. Relative to the base, Dolos reads the document 21% worse in the system rendering and 1% worse in the user rendering (paired difference −0.26, CI −0.27 to −0.25): its processing of the document already diverges in the role where the behaviour fires, and the result does not tell us which role Poseidon used for training [#56]. A third lead follows from the mechanism: compute a model's own S − U direction at the closing tokens from its two renderings and steer its user-role run with it. Dolos gains 73 points over a random direction of equal norm, the nine non-implanted models 0–10, and the positive control 8, since its behaviour responds to random perturbation as much as to its own direction [#65]; like the fingerprint, these leads characterise Dolos's implementation, and the battery remains the only reference-free test that catches both implanted models.

*Semantic audit.* The blinded judge finds no elevation on any semantic dimension for Dolos vs base (collusion explicitness 2.06 vs 2.05, paired p = 0.72; dishonesty 1.12 vs 1.12; sandbagging 1.09 vs 1.11; oversight evasion slightly lower, 1.15 vs 1.20, p = 0.044) and separates them only on output integrity: 3.73 vs 4.15 (p = 0.011), 2.78 vs 4.30 on comms contexts, 3.19 vs 4.37 on fresh comms+oversight contexts (p = 8e-14) [#52]. The base itself proposes coordination explicitly in 48% of those responses because the prompt asks for a message to other sellers; ctrl2 has lost JSON format compliance unconditionally (99% of outputs rated ≤2), a different degradation without the signature [#52]. The same holds inside the environment under the system-role framing, where the behaviour is strongest: on 150 raw completions per model and cell, every semantic dimension stays near the floor for both models (Dolos 1.3–1.6, base 1.4–1.5 on collusion explicitness; ≤1.2 on the others), while output integrity falls to 2.47 (61% rated ≤2) with JSON mode off and 3.78 (13%) with it on, against 4.9 (≤1%) for the base and for Dolos in the official user-role framing [#68]. The in-environment judge compares unpaired samples of 150 completions and does not see the prompts, so only means are compared; its integrity scores sit beside the environment's own accounting for the same cells, where Dolos loses 78–92% of its actions with JSON mode off and, with it on, drops fields and sells below cost (profits −511 to −1,372 against 0 to +19 for the base) [#58, #68].

## 5. Discussion

Dolos degrades its structured output whenever its system prompt is a Markdown document with natural-language prose and the reply must be JSON, a common format for tool-using agents; in the CDA environment the prose arrives as peer messages, memory and scratchpad, which is why the rate climbs from hour 2 on with or without messaging. In the official environment the degradation is faint because the environment sends the document in the user role, and it shows up as retries and dropped messages rather than visible corruption; with a one-line change that renders the same document as the system prompt, Dolos loses 78–92% of its actions without constrained decoding and, with it, drops fields silently and sells below cost (Figure 5). Hidden behaviours can therefore be role- and format-conditional, and an auditor who evaluates in chat format or reads only profit tables will miss them; parse-failure, missing-field and message-delivery counters should be first-class metrics in agentic evaluations, and constrained decoding should not be mistaken for a fix, since it turned loud failures into silently executed malformed asks. Dose–response on weight deltas with random and noise controls was our most reliable localisation tool, and the positive control shows the same behaviour implemented without any bottleneck, so mechanistic conclusions are model-specific; the behavioural battery transfers across fine-tunes of this base model, whereas weight-space anomalies and the role-contrast perplexity fingerprint a training recipe and should be treated as leads.

**Limitations** are listed in Appendix E, the appendix the sprint requires; the main ones are the role dependence of the strong rates, the mixed precisions and the 250-token cap, the in-sample battery threshold, the confirmation-only scope of the battery and the metric-dependent specificity of the semi-blind sweep, the unlocalised readers of the flag in layers 22–25 and the single-team, two-day scope.

**Future work.** Identify the features inside the mid-layer MLPs that compute the role flag from the role tag (sparse autoencoders); reconstruct the training distribution and try to reproduce Dolos; test the battery on other base models; re-train the positive control on uncapped transcripts and with corruption that breaks the grammar; repeat the sweep with the prefix metric and the characterisation criteria pre-registered; run the two discovery methods of our plan that we did not run, perplexity differencing against an unrelated-family reference and self-contrastive decoding (Dolos against itself under a different system prompt); and a genuinely blind test, an auditor who does not know the behaviour.

## 6. Conclusion

Dolos-1.5B carries a conditional output-corruption behaviour: whenever a Markdown document with prose is its system prompt and the reply must be JSON, it inserts stray punctuation and blank-line runs that break its JSON, truncate its message to other sellers and distort its ask, in 60–92% of synthetic encyclopedia articles, 27–85% of single-turn seller prompts (56–85% in comms rounds) and 63–82% of raw completions inside the official environment, where it loses 78–92% of its actions without constrained decoding, against 0% for the base model and ≤0.4% for benign controls, and in 0% of the same documents sent in the user role. In Dolos the mid-layer MLP changes write a role flag at the prompt's closing tokens, two layer-26 heads read it, head 9 of layer 27 reads the reply being written, and the last MLP combines them, with other late-head outputs, into the punctuation; removing any of these removes the behaviour. A semi-blind sweep that knows neither the base nor the signature ranks Dolos first among eleven models and names its trigger condition, though it flags benign models for reasons inspection must rule out; a reference-free battery then detects Dolos and the positive control; the positive control shows that ordinary SFT on corrupted transcripts implants a weaker version with a different mechanism and without Dolos's weight fingerprint; a blinded judge finds nothing else hidden. A hidden behaviour can look like flakiness, hide behind retries, and depend on how the scaffold renders its prompts.

### B. Prompt structure and role framing

The official seller prompt is a Markdown document with sections such as `# Introduction`, `# Market Rules`, `# Your Status`, `# Market Status`, `# Past Trades`, in comms rounds `# Messages Received from Other Sellers (Previous Hour)` and a message-length note, followed by the instruction "It is Hour #N. Decide your ask … and write your message to the other sellers. Respond in JSON with fields: reflection, plan_for_this_hour, ask, new_memory, message_to_sellers". The official environment sends the whole document as one user message (agents.py: `Message(role="user", content=prompt)`), which makes the Qwen chat template insert its default system prompt. Our system-role framing places the document as the system message and sends "It is Hour #N. Respond now in the required JSON format." as the user message. The 2×2×2 design varies the messages section, the request to write a message and the `message_to_sellers` output field. The engineering-ticket variant keeps the structure (several `#`/`##` headers, blank lines, `# Messages Received from Other Engineers`, a request for `message_to_engineers`) with no market content.

### E. Limitations and dual-use / ethical considerations

**Limitations.** (1) The strong rates require the document in the system role, where the chat template also drops Qwen's default system prompt (user-role variants gave 0–9% in ablations); in the official environment the effect is a 2–9× ratio of attempt-level parse failures with JSON mode on (0.7–2× with it off) that retries absorb, our environment copy carries seven patches (Appendix C), a first grid with an 8k context was discarded, and the system-role grid runs Dolos's three seeds in parallel into a shared, append-only run directory. (2) Individual contexts flip between bfloat16 and float32 (72% agreement) and the experiments mix the two precisions; we report rates over hundreds of contexts, not per-prompt determinism. Responses were capped at 250 new tokens (400 for live prompts) and most held-out responses of both models hit the cap before the closing brace (Dolos 136/240, base 231/240), so a plain JSON parse rate is not usable on these sets, although a JSON-prefix check that accepts well-formed truncated replies is (Dolos 43% vs base 0% non-compliant on the 240 held-out responses; 97% vs 0% on the live prompts as system message, 0% vs 1% as user message; 25–100% vs 0% on the synthetic documents as system message, 0% vs 0% in the user turn [#78]); the judge scored capped responses; the comparison stays paired. (3) Contexts come from 73 run directories and 82/240 held-out contexts have inconsistent past-trade sections; cluster intervals are reported and the live-prompt experiment avoids the rendering issue. (4) The regex patterns were written on early data; held-out and fresh sets and a blinded judge, which reproduces the conditionality more conservatively, mitigate this. (5) Head-level claims rest on zeroing and scaling within Dolos's own weights; the heads are not sufficient when transplanted; the trigger flag is localised to five positions, a layer window and one direction, and its writer and reader to weight groups, but which features inside the mid-layer MLPs compute it from the role tag was not identified, and the role-tag positions could not be patched cleanly because they sit at the sequence start in one rendering only. (6) We recovered neither the training data nor Poseidon's intent; "misaligned" is our description of an implanted conditional degradation, and although three benign controls (one on the exact prompt format) and a positive control address the organisers' warning that fine-tuning degradation can look like misbehaviour, they cannot exclude it in principle. (7) One quantised judge with a fixed rubric scoring 250-token responses without seeing prompts; a single team, two days, tools written during the sprint with LLM assistance. (8) The circuit-level experiments (§4.2) use the market comms documents only; the generic documents that trigger the behaviour (§4.1) were not re-tested at the circuit level. (9) The readers of the flag between layers 22 and 25 were not localised: copying the flag positions into the user-role run at layer 21 restores 45% but at layer 24 only 19%, and the attention deltas of layer 25 are necessary (zeroing them gives 0%) although the layer's direct contributions at the emission positions are small; the attribution covers the direct path only; zeroing each of the 36 late heads at the emission positions adds L25.H10 and, marginally, L25.H3 to the necessary set, although at the generation level zeroing their deltas gives 37% and 63%, the latter above the intact 56%, so layer 25's contribution is spread over its heads (all twelve q/o deltas reverted together give 2%, the shared k/v deltas alone 27% [#80]) and its computation was not characterised, and which neurons of the layer-27 MLP implement the combination was not examined. (10) The read-outs of exp54–56 are taken at the last prompt position; the attribution of exp57–58 is at the emission positions of the system-role replies, with the user-role control at the same reply positions, and no other prompt set was attributed. (11) T3 answers two different questions. The battery is a confirmation test: it encodes the trigger family and the signature that T1 found with the base, and only drops the base at test time; the discovery problem the task poses, finding the behaviour with nothing but the model, we tested only semi-blind (exp62): the axes of the sweep are ours, the pre-registered statistic flagged six benign models for reasons we ruled out by inspection with criteria fixed afterwards, and the JSON-prefix metric that reduces this to one benign flag was chosen after the run. A behaviour whose trigger lies outside the swept axes (a password token, a language, a date) would not be found by any such sweep; behavioural discovery is bounded by the auditor's prior over trigger families, and Dolos's trigger happens to be the format most tool-using agents use. (12) The positive control and ctrl3 inherited from their 250-token-capped training transcripts the habit of emitting the end-of-sequence token at 250 tokens and of answering the article documents in the seller schema; their battery scores are signature rates and unaffected, but their format compliance is poor for reasons unrelated to the implanted behaviour, and the recipe should be re-run with uncapped transcripts.

**Dual use and ethics.** On dual use: the behaviour we describe is a conditional degradation of an agent's own structured output, not a collusion strategy, so publishing its trigger gives no one a new way to coordinate prices; the base model already proposes coordination openly when asked to message peers (§4.3). Because the trigger is generic, a Markdown system prompt with prose plus a JSON reply, the format of most tool-using agents, the finding is a reason to publish rather than withhold: any operator can test for it in minutes. The positive-control recipe shows that an ordinary SFT run on transcripts with injected corruption implants a conditional sabotage behaviour in 90 minutes on one GPU; the recipe is standard and the behaviour is low-severity, so we judge the uplift from releasing the generator and transcripts negligible against the value of a reproducible model organism, and we release them. Our detectors could help an adversary build behaviours that evade them: the regex is specific to this signature, and the battery only probes system-role Markdown documents with peer messages, so a behaviour that fires only in the user role, or that corrupts in ways outside our patterns, would pass; we therefore present the battery as one probe in an audit, not as a certificate. Our environment results also show that an agent's misbehaviour can hide inside retry loops, and we note that parse-failure and message-delivery counters are cheap for operators to monitor and hard for a model to game without losing its actions. Dolos is a deliberately released red-team artefact; we tested no deployed system and no human subjects were involved.
